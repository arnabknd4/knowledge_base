## 1. Learn It (Core Understanding)
- **One-line definition:** Narrow AI (ANI) is AI built to perform specific tasks or a limited set of tasks, while Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) is hypothetical AI that can learn and perform any intellectual task a human can, across domains.
- **Plain-English explanation (3-4 lines):**
  Every AI system in real use today is narrow: it can be superb at a defined job (translation, fraud detection, writing assistance) but does not have human-like general understanding or the ability to transfer skills freely to any new situation. AGI is the goal of a system that could learn a new domain, reason, and adapt like a person. It does not exist today, and there is no agreed definition or timeline for it. Artificial Superintelligence (ASI) goes further still: AI that surpasses human ability across nearly all domains.
- **Why it exists / problem it solves:**
  - Gives a shared vocabulary for what AI can do now vs what it might do in future
  - Helps separate marketing hype from real capability
  - Frames long-term safety and policy debates (alignment, control, societal impact)
- **Key terms to know:**
  - **Narrow AI / ANI / Weak AI:** Task-specific AI (all current systems)
  - **AGI / Strong AI / General AI:** Hypothetical human-level, cross-domain intelligence
  - **ASI:** Hypothetical intelligence exceeding human capability broadly
  - **Transfer learning:** Applying knowledge from one task to another (limited in narrow AI)
  - **General-purpose AI (GPAI):** Regulatory term for models usable across many tasks (e.g., LLMs). It is **not** the same as AGI
  - **Singularity:** Speculative point where AI self-improvement outpaces human control or understanding
  - **Alignment:** Making AI goals and behaviour match human intent (central to AGI/ASI safety debate)
- **How it works (simple steps):** (how to place any system on the spectrum)
  1. Ask: is it built or trained for a defined task or domain? → **Narrow AI**
  2. Ask: can it autonomously learn and perform *any* human intellectual task with human-level flexibility? → **AGI** (none exist today)
  3. Ask: does it far exceed the best humans across nearly all fields? → **ASI** (hypothetical)
- **Where it sits in the AI stack:** Conceptual classification layer. It describes capability level, not a technology layer, and it frames risk discussions at the governance layer.

## 2. Mental Model
- **Analogy:** Narrow AI is a **world-class specialist** (a chess grandmaster or a heart surgeon): unbeatable in one field, helpless outside it. AGI would be a **capable, adaptable human professional** who can pick up almost any job. Today's AI is many specialists, some with impressively wide skills, but none with true all-round flexibility.
- **Diagram / flow:**
```
  Narrow AI (ANI)  ──────►  AGI  ──────►  ASI
  Exists today             Hypothetical    Hypothetical
  Task-specific            Human-level     Beyond human
                           across domains  across domains
```
- **The one thing to remember:** All AI today is narrow, even powerful LLMs. AGI is a goal and a debate, not a product.
- **Compare and contrast:**

  | | Narrow AI | AGI |
  |---|---|---|
  | Status | Exists and widely deployed | Hypothetical, no consensus on definition or timeline |
  | Scope | Specific task(s) or domain | Any intellectual task |
  | Adaptability | Limited transfer to new tasks | Human-like learning and transfer |
  | Examples | Spam filter, recommendation engine, chess engine, LLM chatbot | None yet (science fiction or research goal) |
  | Governance focus | Bias, accuracy, privacy, misuse, accountability | Alignment, control, existential and societal risk (debated) |

## 3. 🎯 Exam Focus
- **Likely question types:** Definition / Compare / Best-fit / Identify-the-myth
- **Must-memorize facts:**
  - [ ] Narrow AI = task-specific; it is the **only** kind in use today
  - [ ] AGI = human-level across domains; hypothetical
  - [ ] ASI = surpasses humans broadly; hypothetical
  - [ ] Synonyms: Narrow = Weak AI; General = Strong AI
  - [ ] LLMs and chatbots are still classified as narrow AI (broad, but not AGI)
  - [ ] "General-purpose AI" (regulatory term) ≠ AGI
  - [ ] Narrow AI can outperform humans at its specific task and still be narrow
- **Keywords that signal this topic in a question:** "any intellectual task," "human-level," "weak/strong AI," "task-specific," "hypothetical," "superintelligence"
- **Commonly confused with:**
  - Narrow AI vs "weak" (weak refers to scope, not quality)
  - GPAI (EU AI Act term) vs AGI
  - LLM versatility vs AGI
  - AGI vs ASI
- **Elimination tips (how to rule out wrong options):**
  - Reject options stating AGI is already deployed or commercially available.
  - Reject "narrow AI means low-performing or simple." Narrow refers to scope.
  - Reject "ChatGPT/Claude-type models are AGI" as a settled fact. Mainstream classification treats them as narrow (or at most, broad narrow) AI.
  - If a scenario describes a system doing one defined business task, the answer is narrow AI.
- **Frameworks / standards link:**
  - **EU AI Act:** Regulates by **risk tier** and use case, plus obligations for **general-purpose AI models**. It does not regulate "AGI" as a category, and GPAI is about model versatility, not human-level intelligence.
  - **NIST AI RMF:** Addresses risks of AI systems that exist today; AGI is not a defined scope.
  - **ISO/IEC 42001:** Applies to the AI systems an organisation actually develops or uses (all narrow today).
  - **Governance angle:** Practical assurance work focuses on narrow AI risks (bias, reliability, privacy, accountability). AGI appears mainly in safety research and policy debate.

| # | Scenario | Industry | How this topic applies | Outcome / risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fraud detection model | Banking | Narrow AI excels at one task | High accuracy in scope; fails on unseen fraud patterns or other tasks |

- **Consulting / assurance lens:**
  - Clients may over-estimate capability ("the AI can do anything") or raise AGI fears. Bring the conversation back to the actual, narrow system and its specific risks.
- **Evidence or controls you would look for:**
  - Clearly documented intended purpose and limits of each AI system
  - Testing performed within and outside the intended scope
  - Human oversight matched to real (narrow) capability, not marketing claims

## 5. Q&A Bank

### Quick recall
- **Q:** What is Narrow AI?
  **A:** AI designed for specific tasks or a limited domain; all AI in use today.

- **Q:** What is AGI?
  **A:** Hypothetical AI with human-level ability to learn and perform any intellectual task across domains.

- **Q:** Does AGI exist today?
  **A:** No.

### Conceptual
- **Q:** Are LLMs AGI?
  **A:** No. They are versatile and can handle many language tasks, but they are still trained systems with known limits (hallucination, weak reliability on novel reasoning, no persistent autonomous learning). They are generally classified as narrow AI, and "general-purpose AI" in regulation refers to versatility, not human-level intelligence.

- **Q:** Can a narrow AI be better than humans?
  **A:** Yes, at its specific task (e.g., chess, image classification). Narrow refers to scope, not performance.

- **Q:** Why does the distinction matter for governance?
  **A:** It keeps risk work grounded. Current controls address real, present risks of narrow systems, while AGI concerns (alignment, control) shape long-term safety research and policy.

### Scenario-based
- **Q:** A vendor says its new model "is basically AGI." How should an assurance professional respond?
  **A:** Treat it as a marketing claim. Ask for evidence, define what capabilities are actually demonstrated, assess the system as a narrow AI with its documented limits, and apply normal risk controls.

### Interview-style
- **Q:** "Explain narrow AI vs AGI to a non-technical executive."
  **A:** "Every AI we use today is like a very talented specialist: great at the job it was built or trained for, but it doesn't truly understand the world like a person. AGI would be an AI that can learn and do almost any intellectual job like a human. It doesn't exist yet, so our focus should be managing the real risks of the specialist tools we actually use."

## 6. ✅ Dos and ❌ Don'ts

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Classify current systems as narrow AI | Claim or assume a product is AGI |
| Define each system's intended purpose and limits | Assume AI can handle tasks it was not built or tested for |
| Separate hype from demonstrated capability | Accept vendor "human-like" claims without evidence |
| Keep "general-purpose AI" (regulatory) distinct from AGI | Use GPAI and AGI interchangeably |

- **Common mistakes / misconceptions:**
  1. "Weak AI means bad AI." (It means narrow scope.)
  2. "ChatGPT-style models are AGI." (Not by mainstream classification.)
  3. "AGI is a few products away and already regulated as a category." (No agreed definition or timeline; regulation targets uses and GPAI models.)
- **Red flags in practice:**
  - Vendor or internal claims of human-like general intelligence
  - Oversight designed for imagined, not actual, capability
