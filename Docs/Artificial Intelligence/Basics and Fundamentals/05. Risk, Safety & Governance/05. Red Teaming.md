## 1. Learn It (Core Understanding)
- **One-line definition:** Red teaming is the practice of stress-testing AI systems by intentionally probing for failures, risks, and unsafe behaviors.
- **Plain-English explanation (3–4 lines):**  
  Red teaming means acting like an adversary to find weaknesses in an AI system. Instead of testing whether the AI works in normal conditions, red teams try to break it — by pushing edge cases, adversarial prompts, or unsafe scenarios. This helps uncover vulnerabilities before deployment.
- **Why it exists / problem it solves:**  
  - Identifies risks that normal testing misses  
  - Exposes bias, hallucination, or unsafe outputs  
  - Improves robustness and trustworthiness of AI systems  
- **Key terms to know:**  
  - **Adversarial testing:** Deliberately trying to break the system  
  - **Prompt injection:** Manipulating inputs to bypass safeguards  
  - **Safety stress test:** Evaluating AI under worst-case scenarios  
  - **Mitigation:** Fixes applied after red team findings  
- **How it works (simple steps):**  
  1. Define risk scenarios (bias, unsafe outputs, misuse).  
  2. Create adversarial prompts or inputs.  
  3. Run AI system under stress conditions.  
  4. Document failures and risks.  
  5. Apply mitigations and re-test.  
- **Where it sits in the AI stack:** Safety and governance layer, alongside evals and monitoring.

## 2. Mental Model
- **Analogy:** Think of red teaming like a “penetration test” for AI. Just as cybersecurity experts try to hack systems to find vulnerabilities, red teams try to trick AI into unsafe or biased outputs.  
- **Diagram / flow:**
    - AI system → Adversarial prompts → Failures identified → Mitigation → Safer system
- **The one thing to remember:** Red teaming = adversarial stress-testing to uncover risks before deployment.  
- **Compare and contrast:**

| | Normal evals | Red teaming |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Accuracy, performance | Safety, robustness, misuse |
| Inputs | Standard benchmarks | Adversarial, edge-case prompts |
| Outcome | Scores, metrics | Risk reports, mitigations |

## 5. Q&A Bank
- **Q:** What is red teaming in AI?  
  **A:** Stress-testing AI systems with adversarial inputs to uncover risks and unsafe behaviors.  

- **Q:** How does red teaming differ from evals?  
  **A:** Evals use standardized benchmarks; red teaming uses adversarial, edge-case testing.  

- **Q:** What is prompt injection?  
  **A:** Manipulating inputs to bypass safeguards and force unsafe outputs.  

- **Q:** Why is red teaming important?  
  **A:** It uncovers vulnerabilities before deployment, improving safety and trust.

## 6. ✅ Dos and ❌ Don’ts
| ✅ Do | ❌ Don’t |
|---|---|
| Use red teaming before deployment | Confuse with normal evals |
| Document risks and mitigations | Assume AI is safe without stress-testing |
| Combine with monitoring post-deployment | Rely only on benchmarks |
| Include diverse perspectives in red teams | Ignore cultural or ethical risks |

- **Common mistakes:**  
  1. Thinking red teaming = cybersecurity only  
  2. Assuming evals alone are enough  
  3. Ignoring human oversight in red team findings
