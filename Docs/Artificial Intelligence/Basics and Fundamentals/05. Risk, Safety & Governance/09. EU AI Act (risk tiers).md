## 1. Learn It (Core Understanding)
- **One-line definition:** The EU AI Act classifies AI systems into risk tiers, with stricter rules for higher-risk categories.
- **Plain-English explanation (3–4 lines):**  
  The EU AI Act is Europe’s landmark regulation for AI. It doesn’t treat all AI equally — instead, it groups systems into risk tiers. The higher the risk to people’s safety or rights, the stricter the requirements. Some uses are banned outright, while others must meet transparency, oversight, and compliance standards.
- **Why it exists / problem it solves:**  
  - Protects fundamental rights and safety  
  - Provides clear rules for AI developers and users  
  - Builds trust in AI adoption across the EU  
- **Key terms to know:**  
  - **Unacceptable risk:** AI uses banned outright (e.g., social scoring, manipulative systems)  
  - **High risk:** Strict requirements (e.g., healthcare, law enforcement, critical infrastructure)  
  - **Limited risk:** Transparency obligations (e.g., chatbots must disclose they are AI)  
  - **Minimal risk:** Few or no obligations (e.g., spam filters, video games)  

---

## 2. Mental Model
- **Analogy:** Think of the EU AI Act like a traffic law. Dangerous vehicles (unacceptable risk) are banned, heavy trucks (high risk) need strict checks, regular cars (limited risk) need transparency, and bicycles (minimal risk) are free to ride.  
- **Diagram / flow:**
    - Unacceptable → High → Limited → Minimal
- **The one thing to remember:** EU AI Act = tiered risk framework for regulating AI.  
- **Compare and contrast:**

| Risk Tier | Examples | Obligations |
|---|---|---|
| Unacceptable | Social scoring, manipulative toys | Banned |
| High | Medical AI, hiring tools, policing | Strict compliance, oversight |
| Limited | Chatbots, deepfakes | Transparency notices |
| Minimal | Spam filters, games | No major obligations |

- **Keywords:** "EU AI Act," "risk tiers," "unacceptable," "high risk," "transparency"  
- **Commonly confused with:**  
  - EU AI Act vs NIST AI RMF (law vs voluntary framework)

  **A:** Unacceptable, High, Limited, Minimal.  

- **Q:** Give one example of unacceptable risk AI.  
  **A:** Social scoring systems.  

- **Q:** What obligations apply to high-risk AI?  
  **A:** Strict compliance, documentation, oversight, risk management.  

- **Q:** How does limited risk differ from minimal risk?

| Classify AI systems by risk tier | Assume all AI has same rules |
| Apply transparency for limited risk | Ignore disclosure obligations |

| Ban unacceptable risk systems | Assume they can be regulated |

- **Common mistakes:**  
  1. Thinking EU AI Act = voluntary guidance  
  2. Confusing limited risk with minimal risk  
  3. Forgetting unacceptable risk = banned outright
