# Govern paved-road templates

Syllabus objective: Offer paved-road templates while preserving clear ownership, versioning, and exceptions.

## What

A paved road is an easy, maintained route to supported outcomes, with transparent alternatives for justified needs.

## Why

Adoption depends on trust: opaque mandates are forked, while unsupported templates invite unsafe workarounds.

## How

Assign product ownership and support; publish compatibility, changelog, deprecation, and exception policies; gather friction feedback; measure task completion, upgrade lag, and support burden.

## Features

Use recurring exceptions as evidence to improve a broadly useful path, not merely to tighten enforcement.

## Code snippets (if any)

No snippet required: template and operating decisions must reflect your actual build system, team ownership, and runtime.

## Do's and Don'ts

DO verify assumptions with workload owners, document ownership, and test the proposed path in a disposable environment. DON’T equate a template, restart policy, managed label, or successful build with a secure and recoverable service.

## Real-life implementation

Ask two teams to onboard using the path. Measure setup time, record undocumented assumptions and recurring exceptions, then propose an owner, review cadence, and one template improvement.

## Q&A

- Who owns a template? A named team accountable for support and lifecycle.
- Should exceptions be permanent? Prefer reviewable, time-bounded decisions.
- What signals a poor paved road? Stale versions, forks, confusion, and workarounds.
