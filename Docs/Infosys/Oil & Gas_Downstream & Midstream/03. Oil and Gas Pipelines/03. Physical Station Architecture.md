## 1. Learn It (Core Understanding)
- **One-line definition:**  
  Physical station architecture refers to the facilities along pipeline networks that maintain flow, measure volumes, and manage custody transfer of hydrocarbons.

- **Plain-English explanation (3-4 lines):**  
  Pipelines require supporting stations to keep hydrocarbons moving safely and efficiently. Pump stations move liquids, compressor stations pressurize gases, metering racks measure flow and quality, and custody transfer systems ensure accurate ownership handover. Together, they form the operational backbone of midstream logistics.

- **Why it exists / problem it solves:**  
  Ensures continuous flow, accurate measurement, and transparent commercial transactions.

- **Key terms to know:**  
  - **Pump Stations:** Increase pressure for liquid pipelines.  
  - **Compressor Stations:** Maintain pressure for gas pipelines.  
  - **Metering Racks:** Measure flow, quality, and volume.  
  - **Custody Transfer:** Legal/financial handover of hydrocarbons.  

- **How it works (simple steps):**  
  1. Pump/compressor stations maintain flow.  
  2. Metering racks measure and record volumes.  
  3. Custody transfer validates ownership and billing.  

- **Where it sits in the AI stack:** Data (flow rates, pressure) → Model (hydraulic optimization) → Application (station ops) → Governance (custody standards).  

- **Related topics:** Pipeline Integrity, Custody Transfer Standards

## 3. 🎯 Exam Focus
- **Likely question types:** Identify station types, explain custody transfer, scenario-based flow assurance.  
- **Must-memorize facts:**  
  - Pump = liquids.  
  - Compressor = gases.  
  - Metering racks = measurement.  
  - Custody transfer = ownership handover.  
- **Keywords that signal this topic in a question:** Pump, compressor, metering, custody transfer.  
- **Commonly confused with:** Refinery units (stations are transport-related, not conversion).  
- **Elimination tips:** If question mentions ownership handover, it’s custody transfer.  
- **Frameworks / standards link:** **API MPMS (Manual of Petroleum Measurement Standards)**, **ISO 5167 (flow measurement)**.
