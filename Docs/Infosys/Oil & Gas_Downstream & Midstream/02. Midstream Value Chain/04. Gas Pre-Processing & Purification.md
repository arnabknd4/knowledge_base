## 1. Learn It (Core Understanding)
- **One-line definition:**  
  Gas pre-processing and purification remove water, acid gases, and impurities from natural gas streams before transmission or refining.

- **Plain-English explanation (3-4 lines):**  
  Raw natural gas contains water vapor, hydrogen sulfide, carbon dioxide, and other impurities. Pre-processing ensures the gas is safe, non-corrosive, and market-ready. Techniques include dehydration, amine sweetening, and impurity removal systems. This step stabilizes gas for transport and downstream use.

- **Why it exists / problem it solves:**  
  Prevents pipeline corrosion, ensures safety, and meets market specifications.

- **Key terms to know:**  
  - **Dehydration:** Removing water vapor (glycol units, molecular sieves).  
  - **Amine Sweetening:** Removing H₂S and CO₂ using amine solutions.  
  - **Impurity Removal:** Filtering particulates, mercury, nitrogen.  

- **How it works (simple steps):**  
  1. Dehydrate gas to remove water vapor.  
  2. Sweeten gas to remove acid gases.  
  3. Filter impurities for market specifications.  

- **Where it sits in the AI stack:** Data (gas composition) → Model (process optimization) → Application (pipeline ops) → Governance (safety standards).  

- **Related topics:** NGL Fractionation, Pipeline Integrity

## 2. Mental Model
- **Analogy:** "Gas purification is like water treatment: you remove dirt, chemicals, and pathogens before it’s safe to drink."  

- **Text Diagram / flow:**  
  Raw Gas → Dehydration → Amine Sweetening → Impurity Removal → Transmission  

- **The one thing to remember:**  
  Purification = dehydration + sweetening + impurity removal.  

- **Compare and contrast:**

| | Gas Pre-Processing | Refining |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Stabilize raw gas | Convert hydrocarbons into fuels |
| Focus | Remove water, acid gases, impurities | Chemical conversion |
| Key difference | Safety & transport readiness | Consumer product creation |

## 3. 🎯 Exam Focus
- **Likely question types:** Identify purification steps, scenario-based corrosion prevention, compare dehydration vs sweetening.  
- **Must-memorize facts:**  
  - Dehydration = glycol/molecular sieve.  
  - Amine sweetening = removes H₂S + CO₂.  
  - Impurity removal = particulates, mercury.  
- **Keywords that signal this topic in a question:** Dehydration, sweetening, impurities, corrosion.  
- **Commonly confused with:** Refining (focus is conversion, not stabilization).  
- **Elimination tips:** If question mentions H₂S removal, it’s sweetening.  
- **Frameworks / standards link:** **API RP 14E**, **ASME B31.8**, **EPA emission standards**.

## 6. ✅ Dos and ❌ Don'ts
| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Dehydrate gas streams | Allow wet gas in pipelines |
| Remove H₂S and CO₂ | Ignore acid gas risks |
| Filter impurities | Send untreated gas downstream |

- **Common mistakes / misconceptions:**  
  1. Confusing sweetening with refining.  
  2. Assuming dehydration alone is sufficient.  

- **Red flags in practice:** Corrosion, untreated sour gas, mercury contamination.
