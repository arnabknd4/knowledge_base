# Decision-Making Processes
## 1. What is it?
- A structured approach to selecting the best course of action among multiple alternatives to achieve objectives.  
- Core Infosys concept: ensures decisions are logical, data‑driven, and strategically aligned.

## 2. Why do we need it?
- Consistency → repeatable framework for decisions.  
- Transparency → accountability and clarity in choices.  
- Risk Reduction → identifies pitfalls before implementation.  
- Strategic Alignment → keeps decisions tied to business goals.

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- Steps: problem identification, information gathering, alternatives, evaluation, selection, implementation, monitoring.  
- Approaches: analytical, collaborative, directive, adaptive.  
- Tools: SWOT, cost‑benefit, decision trees, risk matrices, MCDA.  
- Situational traps: bias, overload, time pressure, stakeholder conflict.  
- Keywords: governance boards, workflows, risk committees.

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
- Enterprise cloud migration decision:  
  - Problem → legacy infrastructure slowing innovation.  
  - Alternatives → rehost, replatform, rearchitect.  
  - Evaluation → cost, risk, scalability.  
  - Decision → rearchitect for microservices.  
  - Outcome → improved agility, reduced long‑term costs.

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: What are the key steps in enterprise decision‑making?  
  A: Identify problem, gather data, evaluate alternatives, select option, implement, monitor outcomes.  
- Q: How do governance boards support decision‑making?  
  A: They formalize approvals, enforce standards, and align decisions with risk and compliance frameworks.

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Relying on intuition instead of structured analysis.  
- Ignoring stakeholder conflicts and consensus building.  
- Overlooking monitoring/review after implementation.  
- Allowing bias or incomplete data to drive choices.

## 7. Mental Model
- Analogy: **“Decision GPS”** — structured processes guide enterprises to the best route among multiple paths.  
- Mnemonic: **“PIIE SIM → Problem, Information, Identify, Evaluate, Select, Implement, Monitor.”**

## 8. Revision Summary
- Decision‑making = structured, logical, data‑driven.  
- Steps → problem → data → alternatives → evaluation → selection → implementation → monitoring.  
- Approaches → analytical, collaborative, directive, adaptive.  
- Tools → SWOT, cost‑benefit, decision trees, MCDA.  
- Exam trap → ignoring bias, stakeholder conflicts, or monitoring.
