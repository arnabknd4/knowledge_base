# LEED – Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design
## 1. What is it?
- A globally recognized certification system for sustainable buildings and data centers.  
- Core Infosys concept: ensures enterprise facilities (including IT infrastructure) meet green design, construction, and operational standards.

## 2. Why do we need it?
- Environmental Sustainability → reduces ecological footprint of enterprise facilities.  
- Compliance → aligns with international green building regulations.  
- Cost Efficiency → lowers energy and operational costs through efficient design.  
- CSR → demonstrates commitment to sustainability and ESG goals.  
- Competitive Advantage → attracts eco‑conscious customers, investors, and employees.

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- Scope: data centers, office buildings, IT facilities.  
- Principles: energy efficiency, water conservation, sustainable materials, indoor environmental quality.  
- Situational traps: treating LEED as a one‑time certification instead of continuous operational compliance.  
- Keywords: green buildings, certification, sustainability, efficiency.

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
- Enterprise building a new data center:  
  - Strategy → design with advanced cooling, renewable energy, sustainable materials.  
  - Outcome → LEED Gold certification, reduced energy bills, enhanced brand reputation.  
- Another case → corporate headquarters redesigned for LEED Platinum, improving employee well‑being and reducing operational costs.

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: What is the purpose of LEED certification in enterprise IT?  
  A: To ensure data centers and facilities meet sustainability standards in design, construction, and operations.  
- Q: How does LEED differ from ISO 14001 or Energy Star?  
  A: LEED focuses on building‑level sustainability, ISO 14001 on organizational environmental management, and Energy Star on product‑level efficiency.

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Viewing LEED as a marketing badge instead of operational discipline.  
- Ignoring ongoing compliance and monitoring requirements.  
- Underestimating upfront investment in sustainable construction.  
- Failing to integrate IT infrastructure needs into building design.

## 7. Mental Model
- Analogy: **“Green Building Passport”** — LEED certifies that enterprise facilities are designed and operated sustainably.  
- Mnemonic: **“EWIS → Energy, Water, Indoor quality, Sustainable materials.”**

## 8. Revision Summary
- LEED = certification for sustainable buildings and data centers.  
- Benefits → sustainability, compliance, cost savings, CSR, competitive advantage.  
- Scope → facilities, IT infrastructure, corporate offices.  
- Exam trap → treating certification as static instead of continuous improvement.
