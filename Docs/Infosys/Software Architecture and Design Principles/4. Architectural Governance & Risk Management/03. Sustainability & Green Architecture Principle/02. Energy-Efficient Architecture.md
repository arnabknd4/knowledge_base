# Energy-Efficient Architecture
## 1. What is it?
- The design and implementation of IT systems and enterprise architecture that minimize energy consumption while maintaining performance and scalability.  
- Core Infosys concept: optimize resources, reduce waste, and align with sustainability goals.

## 2. Why do we need it?
- Environmental Impact → reduces carbon emissions and ecological footprint.  
- Cost Savings → lowers energy bills and operational expenses.  
- Regulatory Compliance → meets global sustainability and green IT standards.  
- Corporate Responsibility → enhances brand reputation and ESG scores.  
- Future Readiness → aligns with global sustainable technology trends.

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- Principles: resource optimization, virtualization/consolidation, cloud adoption, efficient data centers, energy‑aware software design.  
- Strategies: green procurement, monitoring/analytics, automation, lifecycle management.  
- Situational traps: ignoring upfront investment or measurement complexity.  
- Keywords: virtualization, green data centers, auto‑scaling, ISO 14001.

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
- Enterprise adopting virtualization:  
  - Strategy → consolidate servers into VMs and containers.  
  - Outcome → reduced hardware footprint, lower energy bills, improved scalability.  
- Another case → green data centers with advanced cooling and renewable energy integration.

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: Why is energy efficiency critical in enterprise architecture?  
  A: It reduces environmental impact, ensures compliance, lowers costs, and strengthens brand reputation.  
- Q: How can enterprises implement energy‑efficient strategies?  
  A: Through virtualization, cloud adoption, green procurement, monitoring tools, and lifecycle management.

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Treating energy efficiency as optional instead of strategic.  
- Ignoring redesign needs for legacy systems.  
- Underestimating upfront investment in green technologies.  
- Difficulty in quantifying energy savings due to poor metrics.

## 7. Mental Model
- Analogy: **“Lean Engine”** — energy‑efficient architecture is like tuning an engine to deliver maximum performance with minimum fuel.  
- Mnemonic: **“RVCEE → Resource, Virtualization, Cloud, Efficient data centers, Energy‑aware design.”**

## 8. Revision Summary
- Energy‑efficient architecture = minimize energy use while maintaining performance.

- Strategies → green procurement, monitoring, automation, lifecycle management.  
- Benefits → cost savings, compliance, performance, competitive advantage.
