# Risk Heat Maps
## 1. What is it?
- A visual tool in risk management that represents likelihood vs. impact of risks using a color‑coded grid.  
- Core Infosys concept: simplifies communication, prioritizes risks, and guides mitigation planning.

## 2. Why do we need it?
- Simplifies risk communication for executives and stakeholders.  
- Prioritizes high‑impact, high‑likelihood risks for immediate action.  
- Supports decision‑making by aligning resources with severity.  
- Monitors risk trends over time.

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- Structure: X‑axis = likelihood, Y‑axis = impact.  
- Color coding: green (low), yellow (moderate), orange (significant), red (high).  
- Situational traps: subjectivity in scoring, static representation, oversimplification.  
- Keywords: probability, severity, prioritization, visualization.

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
- Cybersecurity risk management:  
  - Risks → phishing attacks, ransomware, insider threats.  
  - Heat map → phishing (high likelihood, medium impact = orange), ransomware (medium likelihood, high impact = red).  
  - Outcome → prioritized investment in ransomware defenses and phishing awareness training.

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: Why are risk heat maps effective in enterprise governance?  
  A: They provide visual clarity, quick prioritization, and strategic alignment of mitigation strategies.  
- Q: What are the limitations of risk heat maps?  
  A: Subjectivity in scoring, static nature, and reduction of complex risks into two dimensions.

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Over‑reliance on heat maps without deeper analysis.  
- Treating them as real‑time tools when they are static snapshots.  
- Ignoring assessor bias in scoring.  
- Using them without linking to governance or mitigation plans.

## 7. Mental Model
- Analogy: **“Risk Thermometer”** — heat maps show how hot (critical) each risk is.  
- Mnemonic: **“LIRC → Likelihood, Impact, Risk color.”**

## 8. Revision Summary
- Risk heat maps = visual prioritization tool.  
- Structure → likelihood vs. impact grid with color coding.  
- Benefits → clarity, prioritization, decision support, monitoring.  
- Challenges → subjectivity, static view, oversimplification.  
- Exam trap → treating heat maps as complete risk analysis.
