# Executive Communication & Influence
## 1. What is it?
- The ability of leaders and architects to inspire, persuade, and align stakeholders toward a common vision.  
- Core Infosys concept: clarity, confidence, and strategic messaging that resonates across executives, peers, and teams.

## 2. Why do we need it?
- Ensures technical proposals are understood in business terms.  
- Builds trust and credibility with stakeholders.  
- Reduces conflict and accelerates decision‑making.  
- Positions architects as strategic influencers, not just technical experts.

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- Principles: storytelling, negotiation, conflict resolution.  
- Situational traps: using jargon without context, focusing on positions instead of interests.  
- Keywords: BATNA, storytelling, active listening, win‑win outcomes.

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
- Infosys architect explaining microservices adoption:  
  - Storytelling → “Our customers faced delays during peak hours… the monolith couldn’t scale… microservices enabled faster launches.”  
  - Negotiation → Cloud vs. on‑prem debate resolved with hybrid solution balancing cost and compliance.

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: How would you use storytelling to explain architecture to executives?  
  A: Frame decisions as a narrative: problem → conflict → resolution → impact, using relatable analogies and KPIs.  
- Q: What negotiation techniques help resolve IT vs. business conflicts?  
  A: Prepare BATNA, focus on interests, use data, and propose win‑win solutions.

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Overloading presentations with technical jargon.  
- Ignoring stakeholder emotions and pain points.  
- Treating negotiation as zero‑sum instead of collaborative.  
- Failing to use data and KPIs to justify proposals.

## 7. Mental Model
- Analogy: **“Architect as Storyteller & Diplomat”** — one role explains the vision, the other resolves conflicts.  
- Mnemonic: **“SCAN → Storytelling, Conflict resolution, Active listening, Negotiation.”**

## 8. Revision Summary
- Executive communication = clarity + persuasion.  
- Storytelling makes architecture relatable.  
- Negotiation requires BATNA, trust, win‑win focus.  
- Conflict resolution → active listening + reframing.  
- Use KPIs and data to build credibility.  
- Exam trap: ignoring business language in executive communication.
