# Techniques for Mapping Architecture Decisions to KPIs
## 1. What is it?
- A structured approach to connect architecture choices with measurable business outcomes (KPIs).
- Core Infosys concept: architecture must demonstrate quantifiable alignment with strategic goals.

## 2. Why do we need it?
- Ensures architecture decisions are not abstract but tied to business impact.  
- Provides transparency for stakeholders and accountability for architects.  
- Enables prioritization of investments based on measurable outcomes.  
- Strengthens governance and supports continuous improvement.

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- Principles: objective identification, KPI definition, mapping, monitoring.  
- Situational traps: selecting KPIs without business relevance or failing to monitor outcomes.  
- Keywords: scorecards, dashboards, alignment, continuous monitoring.

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
- Infosys client aiming to reduce operational costs by 20%:  
  - Business objective → cost reduction.  
  - KPI → IT spend per transaction.  
  - Architecture decision → cloud migration to reduce infrastructure costs.  
  - Outcome → measurable efficiency gains tracked via dashboards.

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: How would you use scorecards to align architecture with KPIs?  
  A: Create a matrix linking objectives, KPIs, architecture decisions, and expected impact for stakeholder visibility.  
- Q: Why is continuous monitoring critical in KPI mapping?  
  A: It validates assumptions, tracks progress, and enables corrective action when architecture outcomes diverge from business goals.

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Defining KPIs too broadly or without measurable linkage.  
- Mapping architecture decisions without quantifiable outcomes.  
- Ignoring stakeholder communication tools like scorecards.  
- Treating KPI mapping as one‑time exercise instead of continuous monitoring.

## 7. Mental Model
- Analogy: **“Chain of Impact”** — Business Objective → KPI → Architecture Decision → Measurable Outcome.  
- Mnemonic: **“IDLSC → Identify, Define, Link, Scorecard, Continuous.”**

## 8. Revision Summary
- Step 1: Identify business objectives.  
- Step 2: Define relevant KPIs.  
- Step 3: Link architecture decisions to KPI outcomes.  
- Step 4: Use scorecards for alignment.  
- Step 5: Continuously monitor via dashboards.  
- Exam trap: ignoring measurable linkage or failing to monitor.
