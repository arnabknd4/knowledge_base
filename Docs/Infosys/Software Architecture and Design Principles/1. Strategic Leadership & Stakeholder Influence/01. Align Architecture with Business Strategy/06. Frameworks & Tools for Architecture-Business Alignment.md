# Frameworks & Tools for Architecture-Business Alignment
## 1. What is it?
- Structured methods and tools that ensure architecture decisions align with business goals.  
- Core Infosys concept: frameworks are strategic enablers, not optional add‑ons.

## 2. Why do we need it?
- Provide consistency and transparency in decision‑making.  
- Reduce risk of misalignment between IT and business.  
- Improve communication by translating technical choices into business impact language.  
- Enable measurable outcomes tied to KPIs.

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- Principles: alignment, governance, scorecards, balanced perspectives.  
- Situational traps: ignoring frameworks or treating them as compliance checklists only.  
- Keywords: TOGAF, Balanced Scorecard, Architecture Scorecards, ADM.

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
- Infosys client aiming for 20% cost reduction:  
  - Framework → Architecture Scorecard.  
  - KPI → IT spend per transaction.  
  - Architecture decision → Cloud‑native migration.  
  - Outcome → 30% reduction in infrastructure costs, tracked transparently.

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: How would you use Balanced Scorecard to justify architecture investments?  
  A: Map architecture decisions to financial, customer, internal process, and learning perspectives.  
- Q: Why is TOGAF critical for large enterprises?  
  A: Provides standardized ADM process ensuring traceability from business vision to technology implementation.

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Treating frameworks as optional or purely academic.  
- Over‑focusing on technical evaluation without business linkage.  
- Ignoring stakeholder communication tools like scorecards.  
- Misusing Balanced Scorecard by focusing only on financial metrics.

## 7. Mental Model
- Analogy: **“Three lenses”** — Scorecards (tactical lens), Balanced Scorecard (strategic lens), TOGAF (enterprise lens).  
- Mnemonic: **“SBT → Scorecard, Balanced, TOGAF.”**

## 8. Revision Summary
- Frameworks = enablers of alignment.  
- Architecture Scorecards → tactical KPI mapping.  
- Balanced Scorecard → strategic management across 4 perspectives.  
- TOGAF → standardized enterprise architecture method.  
- Benefits: transparency, accountability, reduced risk.  
- Exam trap: ignoring frameworks or treating them as optional.
