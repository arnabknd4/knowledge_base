# Role of Architects in Business Outcomes
## 1. What is it?
- Architects are strategic enablers who connect technology decisions to business success.  
- Core Infosys concept: architects influence outcomes through advisory, stakeholder engagement, and governance.

## 2. Why do we need it?
- Prevents architecture from being seen as a cost center.  
- Ensures technology investments deliver measurable business impact.  
- Builds trust with stakeholders and aligns IT with business priorities.  
- Strengthens governance and reduces risk of misaligned initiatives.

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- Principles: strategic advisory, stakeholder influence, governance alignment.  
- Situational traps: focusing only on technical design without business linkage.  
- Keywords: roadmap, KPIs, ARB, TOGAF, credibility.

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
- Infosys client expanding globally:  
  - Architect role → recommend cloud‑native architecture for scalability.  
  - Outcome → supports market expansion, reduces infrastructure costs, improves agility.  
- Governance model → ARB ensures new data platform aligns with customer analytics goals.

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: How would you translate business goals into technology roadmaps?  
  A: Map strategic priorities (growth, efficiency) to architecture enablers (cloud, automation, modularity).  
- Q: How do architects influence stakeholders effectively?  
  A: Speak business language, use KPIs, visual scorecards, and case studies to justify decisions.

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Using technical jargon without linking to business outcomes.  
- Ignoring stakeholder pain points and failing to build credibility.  
- Treating governance as a compliance checkbox instead of a strategic enabler.  
- Designing systems without anticipating future needs or regulatory changes.

## 7. Mental Model
- Analogy: **“Architect as Translator”** — converts business vision into technology execution.  
- Mnemonic: **“S‑S‑G → Strategic Advisor, Stakeholder Influence, Governance.”**

## 8. Revision Summary
- Architects = strategic advisors, not just system designers.  
- Translate goals into technology roadmaps.  
- Influence stakeholders with business language + KPIs.  
- Governance models (ARB, TOGAF, scorecards) ensure alignment.  
- Benefits: transparency, reduced risk, measurable impact.  
- Exam trap: ignoring business context in architecture role.

## 9. Outside Exposure
- Consider outside material: Gartner leadership case studies, TOGAF governance practices, Harvard Business Review on IT–business alignment.
