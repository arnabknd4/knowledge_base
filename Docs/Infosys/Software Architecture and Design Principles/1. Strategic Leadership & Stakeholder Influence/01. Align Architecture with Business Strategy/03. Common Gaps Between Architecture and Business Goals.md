# Common Gaps Between Architecture and Business Goals
## 1. What is it?
- Disconnects between enterprise architecture decisions and business objectives.
- Core Infosys concept: architecture must serve measurable business outcomes.

## 2. Why do we need it?
- Identifying gaps prevents wasted investments and misaligned priorities.
- Ensures architecture supports agility, efficiency, and governance.
- Strengthens enterprise resilience and competitive advantage.

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- Principles: alignment, KPI mapping, governance.  
- Situational traps: focusing on technical elegance without business context.  
- Keywords: communication, priorities, technical debt, governance gaps.

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
- Infosys client adopting microservices without clear business need → increased cost and complexity.  
- Proper alignment would tie architecture choice to KPIs like faster time‑to‑market or customer experience improvement.

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: How would you bridge communication gaps between business and IT stakeholders?  
  A: Translate technical decisions into business impact language (e.g., scalability → faster customer onboarding).  
- Q: What happens if KPI mapping is missing in architecture decisions?  
  A: Investments fail to show measurable business value, weakening stakeholder trust.

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Over‑engineering architecture without business justification.  
- Ignoring stakeholder language differences (business outcomes vs. technical jargon).  
- Prioritizing scalability over time‑to‑market without balance.  
- Neglecting governance frameworks.  
- Resistance to change due to legacy systems and culture.

## 7. Mental Model
- Analogy: **“Two parallel tracks”** — business goals and architecture must run together; if one diverges, the train derails.  
- Mnemonic: **“CPKG → Context, Priorities, KPIs, Governance.”**

## 8. Revision Summary
- Gap = misalignment between architecture and business.  
- Causes: lack of context, poor communication, misaligned priorities.  
- KPI mapping is critical.  
- Governance ensures structured alignment.  
- Resistance to change blocks evolution.  
- Exam trap: confusing technical elegance with business relevance.
