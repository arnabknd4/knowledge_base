# Linking Architecture to KPIs
## 1. What is it?
- Establishing measurable connections between architecture decisions and business Key Performance Indicators (KPIs).
- Core Infosys concept: architecture must demonstrate quantifiable business impact.

## 2. Why do we need it?
- Ensures alignment of technology initiatives with strategic objectives.  
- Provides transparency and accountability across teams.  
- Enables data‑driven decision‑making and prioritization of investments.  
- Prevents architecture from being seen as cost‑center rather than value‑driver.

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- Principles: KPI mapping, measurement, accountability.  
- Situational traps: implementing platforms without defining measurable outcomes.  
- Keywords: revenue growth, operational efficiency, customer satisfaction, NPS.

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
- Infosys client adopting automation:  
  - KPI → Operational efficiency (cost reduction, resource utilization).  
  - Architecture → Modular automation reduces overhead, improves process speed.  
- Direct linkage shows ROI and builds stakeholder trust.

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: How would you link architecture decisions to KPIs in a consulting engagement?  
  A: Identify business KPIs (growth, efficiency, satisfaction) and map architecture enablers (scalability, automation, reliability) to each.  
- Q: Why is KPI mapping critical in enterprise architecture?  
  A: It quantifies impact, avoids misalignment, and ensures accountability.

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Treating KPIs as generic metrics without business linkage.  
- Focusing only on technical performance (e.g., latency) without mapping to business outcomes.  
- Ignoring customer‑centric KPIs like NPS.  
- Implementing systems without measurable success criteria.

## 7. Mental Model
- Analogy: **“Compass and Map”** — KPIs are the compass (direction), architecture is the map (path).  
- Mnemonic: **“RAM → Revenue, Automation, Metrics.”**

## 8. Revision Summary
- KPIs = measurable link between goals and performance.  
- Architecture must tie to KPIs.  
- Examples: revenue growth, efficiency, satisfaction.  
- KPI mapping = accountability + transparency.  
- Exam trap: ignoring KPI linkage in architecture decisions.  
- Mnemonic: Compass (KPIs) + Map (Architecture).
