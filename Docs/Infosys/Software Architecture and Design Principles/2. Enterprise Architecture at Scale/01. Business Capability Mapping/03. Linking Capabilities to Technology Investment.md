# Linking Capabilities to Technology Investment
## 1. What is it?
- A strategic practice that connects business capabilities (what the business must do) with technology solutions and spending.  
- Core Infosys concept: every IT dollar must directly support business objectives.

## 2. Why do we need it?
- Avoids waste by eliminating tech spend that doesn’t add value.  
- Improves alignment between IT and business priorities.  
- Enables prioritization of investments for critical capabilities.  
- Provides transparency for stakeholders on how IT supports strategy.

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- Principles: capability mapping, gap analysis, investment alignment.  
- Situational traps: investing in technology trends without linking to business needs.  
- Keywords: ERP, CRM, analytics, overlaps, gaps, prioritization.

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
- Banking client aiming to improve risk management:  
  - Capability → Risk Assessment & Compliance.  
  - Current tech → manual spreadsheets (low maturity).  
  - Investment → AI‑driven risk analytics platform.  
  - Outcome → faster compliance checks, reduced regulatory risk.

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: How would you link technology investments to business capabilities?  
  A: Identify capabilities, map them to supporting applications, analyze gaps/overlaps, and align funding to critical priorities.  
- Q: Why is gap analysis important in capability‑investment mapping?  
  A: It highlights missing or weak technology support for critical capabilities, ensuring investment delivers measurable outcomes.

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Treating capabilities as processes or organizational units.  
- Overlapping systems that duplicate functionality.  
- Ignoring maturity assessment, leading to misaligned investments.  
- Communicating in technical terms without showing business impact.

## 7. Mental Model
- Analogy: **“Capability as Anchor”** — technology investments are ropes tied to anchors; without anchors, ropes drift aimlessly.  
- Mnemonic: **“IMAGO → Identify, Map, Analyze, Gaps, Optimize.”**

## 8. Revision Summary
- Capabilities = stable, business‑focused, tech‑agnostic.  
- Link each capability to enabling technology.  
- Analyze gaps (missing tech) and overlaps (redundant systems).  
- Align investments to critical, low‑maturity capabilities.  
- Communicate value via scorecards and stakeholder reports.  
- Exam trap: investing in tech without capability linkage.
