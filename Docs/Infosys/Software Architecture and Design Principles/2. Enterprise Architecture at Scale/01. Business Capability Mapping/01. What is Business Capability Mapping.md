# Business Capability Mapping
## 1. What is it?
- A strategic planning technique that identifies what an organization must be able to do to achieve its objectives.  
- Core Infosys concept: focuses on **capabilities** (business abilities) rather than processes, structures, or technology.

## 2. Why do we need it?
- Provides a business‑centric view instead of a technology‑centric one.  
- Connects strategy to execution by highlighting critical capabilities.  
- Enables prioritization of investments and transformation planning.  
- Improves communication with executives and stakeholders.

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- Principles: stability, independence, technology‑agnostic nature of capabilities.  
- Situational traps: confusing capabilities with processes or organizational units.  
- Keywords: capability layers, maturity assessment, alignment, prioritization.

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
- Infosys client aiming for global expansion:  
  - Capability → Customer Relationship Management.  
  - Sub‑capability → Customer Analytics.  
  - Technology → Data lake + BI tools.  
  - Outcome → Improved customer insights supporting market expansion.

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: How would you explain the difference between a capability and a process?  
  A: Capability = “what the business must do” (e.g., order fulfillment). Process = “how it is done” (steps, workflows).  
- Q: How does capability mapping support IT investment decisions?  
  A: By linking capabilities to strategic goals, it highlights where technology enablers deliver maximum business impact.

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Treating capabilities as processes or organizational units.  
- Over‑focusing on technology instead of business needs.  
- Ignoring maturity assessment, leading to misaligned investments.  
- Failing to prioritize gaps in critical capabilities.

## 7. Mental Model
- Analogy: **“Capability Map as Blueprint”** — shows what the business must be able to do, independent of how or who does it.  
- Mnemonic: **“SITP → Stable, Independent, Technology‑agnostic, Prioritization.”**

## 8. Revision Summary
- Capability = “what business must do.”  
- Stable, independent, tech‑agnostic.  
- Purpose → alignment, prioritization, transformation planning.  
- Layers → domains → sub‑capabilities.  
- Assess maturity → map to strategy → prioritize gaps.  
- Exam trap: confusing capability with process or org chart.
