# ⚡ High Availability & Types of Replication

## 1. What is it?
High Availability (HA) ensures workloads remain accessible despite failures. Replication is the mechanism that maintains **redundant copies of data/services** across nodes or regions to achieve HA.

## 2. Why do we need it?
- Prevent downtime during hardware, software, or regional failures.  
- Meet **RTO/RPO objectives** for mission‑critical workloads.  
- Ensure compliance with business continuity and disaster recovery (BCDR) policies.  

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- **Replication Types**:  
  - **Locally Redundant Storage (LRS)** → 3 copies in one datacenter.  
  - **Zone Redundant Storage (ZRS)** → copies across availability zones.  
  - **Geo‑Redundant Storage (GRS)** → copies in paired region.  
  - **Read‑Access GRS (RA‑GRS)** → adds read access in secondary region.  
- **HA Services**: Availability Sets, Availability Zones, Azure Site Recovery.  
- **Exam Trap**: Confusing **backup retention** with **replication redundancy**.  

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
A financial services app requires **99.99% uptime**.  
- VMs deployed in **Availability Zones**.  
- Data stored in **RA‑GRS** for cross‑region resilience.  
- ASR configured for failover to secondary region.  

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: *How would you design HA for a multi‑region app?*  
  A: Use **ZRS for storage**, **Availability Zones for compute**, and **ASR for DR failover**.  
- Q: *What’s the trade‑off between LRS and GRS?*  
  A: LRS is cheaper but vulnerable to regional failure; GRS adds resilience but increases latency/cost.  
- Q: *How do replication types affect compliance?*  
  A: GRS/RA‑GRS meet cross‑region resilience requirements; LRS may not satisfy regulatory mandates.  

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Assuming **LRS = disaster recovery** (it’s only local redundancy).  
- Not testing failover scenarios with ASR.  
- Overlooking **latency and cost trade‑offs** in replication choices.  
- Confusing **availability zones** with **geo‑replication**.  

## 7. Mental Model
Think of replication as **“mirrors at different distances”**:  
- LRS = mirror in the same room.  
- ZRS = mirrors in different rooms of the same building.  
- GRS = mirror in another city.  
- RA‑GRS = mirror in another city that you can also look into.  

## 8. Revision Summary
- HA = uptime guarantee, replication = data redundancy.  
- LRS → local copies, ZRS → zone copies, GRS → geo copies, RA‑GRS → geo + read access.  
- Availability Sets/Zones + ASR = compute HA + DR.  
- Exam trap: backup ≠ replication.

# Narrow breakdown study format for Replication Types & HA Services

## 1. Locally Redundant Storage (LRS)
- Scope: Single Datacenter
- Keeps three synchronous copies of data within one datacenter.
- Cheapest redundancy option
- Protects against hardware failure
- Not resilient to datacenter or zone outage

## 2 Zone Redundant Storage (ZRS)
- Scope: Availability Zones
- Replicates data synchronously across three availability zones in a region.
- Protects against zone failures
- Maintains high durability within a region
- Recommended for production workloads needing intra‑region resilience

## 3. Geo‑Redundant Storage (GRS)
- Scope: Paired Region
- Replicates data asynchronously to a secondary paired region.
- Protects against regional outages
- Primary region has 3 copies (LRS), secondary region has 3 copies
- Higher latency for secondary access

## 4. Read‑Access GRS (RA‑GRS)
- Scope: Paired Region + Read
- Extends GRS by allowing read access to secondary region.
- Enables disaster recovery testing
- Secondary region data is read‑only
- Useful for compliance and reporting

## 5. Availability Sets
- Compute HA
- Distributes VMs across multiple fault and update domains.
- Protects against hardware rack failures
- Ensures updates don’t impact all VMs simultaneously
- Minimum 2 VMs required

## 6. Availability Zones
- Compute HA
- Physically separate datacenters within a region.
- Independent power, cooling, networking
- Protects against zone‑level failures
- Recommended for mission‑critical workloads

## 7. Azure Site Recovery (ASR)
- Disaster Recovery
- Orchestrates replication and failover to secondary region.
- Provides business continuity during regional outages
- Supports planned/unplanned failover
- Complements Backup (restore points) with DR (failover orchestration)

## 📌 Architect‑Level Revision Notes
- **LRS** → 3 copies, single datacenter, cheapest, no regional protection.
- **ZRS** → zone‑level resilience, synchronous replication, recommended for production.
- **GRS** → cross‑region replication, asynchronous, disaster recovery.
- **RA‑GRS** → adds read access in secondary region, compliance + DR testing.
- **Availability Sets** → VM distribution across fault/update domains.
- **Availability Zones** → zone‑level compute resilience, independent datacenters.
- **ASR** → orchestrated failover, business continuity, disaster recovery.
