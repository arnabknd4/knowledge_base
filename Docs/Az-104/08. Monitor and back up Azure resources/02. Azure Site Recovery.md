# Azure Site Recovery (ASR)

## 1. What is it?
A disaster recovery service that replicates workloads to a secondary location, enabling failover and business continuity during outages.

## 2. Why do we need it?
Provides resilience against regional failures, ensures compliance with disaster recovery policies, and minimizes downtime for mission‑critical applications.

## 3. Core Exam Focus
- Configure replication for Azure VMs, Hyper‑V, VMware, and physical servers
- Recovery Plans (orchestration of failover/failback)
- Portal tasks: enable replication, test failover, planned/unplanned failover
- CLI/PowerShell: `az site-recovery` commands
- Common trap: confusing ASR (DR) with Azure Backup (data protection)

## 4. Real‑World Use Case
A financial institution replicates workloads from its primary Azure region to a secondary region, ensuring compliance with RTO/RPO requirements and enabling seamless failover during regional outages.

## 5. Interview Lens
- Q: How would you design DR for a multi‑region application?  
  A: Use ASR to replicate workloads to a paired region, define Recovery Plans for orchestration, and test failover regularly.  
- Q: What happens during an unplanned failover?  
  A: ASR initiates replication to the target region, DNS updates may be required, and workloads run from the secondary site until failback.  
- Q: How do you optimize DR costs?  
  A: Replicate only critical workloads, use automation for failover testing, and leverage paired regions to reduce replication overhead.

## 6. Common Mistakes
- Confusing backup with disaster recovery
- Not testing failover scenarios
- Overlooking DNS updates during failover
- Misconfigured replication policies leading to data loss

## 7. Mental Model
Think of ASR as a **“mirror city”** — when the primary city (region) goes dark, the mirrored city lights up instantly to keep life running.

## 8. Revision Summary
- ASR = disaster recovery, not backup  
- Replicates workloads across regions  
- Recovery Plans orchestrate failover/failback  
- Supports Azure VMs, Hyper‑V, VMware, physical servers  
- Test failover regularly to avoid surprises  
- DNS + replication policies are critical
