# Architecture Review Boards
What is an Architecture Review Board?

An Architecture Review Board (ARB) is a formal governance body within an enterprise that oversees and ensures the quality, consistency, and compliance of architectural decisions across projects and systems. It acts as a decision-making authority for architecture-related matters, ensuring alignment with business goals, technology standards, and regulatory requirements.

Purpose of ARBs:

Strategic Alignment: Ensure architecture supports organizational objectives.
Standardization: Enforce enterprise architecture principles and standards.
Risk Management: Identify and mitigate architectural risks early.
Quality Assurance: Validate design decisions for scalability, security, and performance.
Change Control: Review and approve major architectural changes.
Key Responsibilities:

Review Architecture Proposals: Evaluate solution designs for compliance.
Approve Standards & Guidelines: Define and maintain architectural principles.
Monitor Compliance: Ensure projects adhere to approved frameworks.
Facilitate Communication: Act as a bridge between business and technical teams.
Provide Guidance: Offer recommendations for complex architectural challenges.
Composition of an ARB:

Enterprise Architects: Provide strategic oversight.
Solution Architects: Represent project-level designs.
Domain Experts: Specialists in security, data, infrastructure.
Business Stakeholders: Ensure alignment with business needs.
Governance Officers: Monitor compliance and risk.
Typical ARB Process:

Submission: Project teams submit architecture documents for review.
Evaluation: ARB assesses compliance with standards and principles.
Feedback: Recommendations or required changes are communicated.
Approval or Rejection: Decisions are documented and tracked.
Follow-up: Monitor implementation and adherence.
Benefits of ARBs:

Consistency Across Projects: Unified architectural approach.
Reduced Risks: Early detection of design flaws.
Improved Quality: Better scalability, security, and maintainability.
Enhanced Collaboration: Clear communication between teams.
Challenges

Perceived Bureaucracy: Can slow down projects if not managed well.
Resource Intensive: Requires skilled professionals and time.
Resistance to Change: Teams may view ARB as restrictive.
