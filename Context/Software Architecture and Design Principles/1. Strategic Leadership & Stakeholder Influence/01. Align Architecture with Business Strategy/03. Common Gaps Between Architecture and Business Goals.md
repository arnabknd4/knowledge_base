## Common Gaps Between Architecture and Business Goals

Despite best intentions, enterprises often face disconnects between architecture and business objectives. Here are the most common gaps:

### a) Lack of Business Context in Architecture Decisions
- Architects focus on technical elegance rather than business impact.
- Example: Choosing a complex microservices architecture without clear business need, increasing cost and complexity.

### b) Poor Communication Between Business and IT
- Business leaders speak in terms of outcomes (growth, efficiency), while architects speak in technical jargon.
- Result: Stakeholders fail to see the value of architecture decisions.

### c) Misaligned Priorities
- Business wants rapid time-to-market; architecture team prioritizes long-term scalability.
- Without alignment, projects either become rushed (leading to technical debt) or delayed (impacting business goals).

### d) Inadequate KPI Mapping
- Architecture decisions rarely tied to measurable business KPIs.
- Example: Implementing a new data platform without defining how it improves customer experience or revenue.

### e) Governance Gaps
- No structured process to ensure architecture decisions align with strategic objectives.
- Leads to fragmented systems and duplicated efforts.

### f) Resistance to Change
- Legacy systems and cultural inertia prevent architecture from evolving with business needs.
- Result: Business strategy shifts, but technology remains static.
