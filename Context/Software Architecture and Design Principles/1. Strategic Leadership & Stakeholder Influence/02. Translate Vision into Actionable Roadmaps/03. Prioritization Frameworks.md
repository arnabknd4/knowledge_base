# Prioritization Frameworks
When creating a roadmap, not all initiatives can start at once. Organizations have limited resources—time, budget, and talent—so prioritization ensures that the most valuable and impactful initiatives are executed first. 

Below are the key frameworks explained in detail:

1. MoSCoW Method

What It Is:
A simple prioritization technique that categorizes initiatives into four groups:
Must-have: Critical for success; cannot be compromised.
Should-have: Important but not essential immediately.
Could-have: Nice-to-have features; add value but not urgent.
Won’t-have (for now): Deferred or dropped due to low impact or high complexity.
Why It Matters:
Helps teams focus on essentials and avoid scope creep.
Example:
For a digital transformation roadmap:
Must-have: Cloud migration.
Should-have: Microservices adoption.
Could-have: AI-driven personalization.
Won’t-have: Blockchain integration (for now).
2. Value vs. Effort Matrix

What It Is:
A 2x2 matrix that plots initiatives based on business value and implementation effort.
Quadrants:
High Value, Low Effort: Quick wins → Highest priority.
High Value, High Effort: Strategic projects → Plan carefully.
Low Value, Low Effort: Low-hanging fruit → Do if resources allow.
Low Value, High Effort: Avoid or defer.
Why It Matters:
Provides a visual way to identify quick wins and strategic bets.
Example:
Quick win: API integration (high value, low effort).
Strategic bet: Microservices adoption (high value, high effort).
3. Weighted Scoring Model

What It Is:
A quantitative approach where initiatives are scored based on multiple criteria.
Common Criteria:
Strategic alignment.
ROI (Return on Investment).
Risk level.
Complexity.
How It Works:
Assign weights to each criterion (e.g., Strategic alignment = 40%, ROI = 30%, Risk = 20%, Complexity = 10%).
Score each initiative against these criteria.
Calculate weighted total → Highest score = highest priority.
Why It Matters:
Removes subjectivity and provides data-driven prioritization.
Example:
Initiative A: Cloud migration → Score: 85
Initiative B: AI personalization → Score: 70
Initiative C: Blockchain → Score: 40
4. Kano Model (Optional Advanced Framework)

What It Is:
Categorizes features based on customer satisfaction impact:
Basic Needs: Must-have for customer acceptance.
Performance Needs: Directly improve satisfaction.
Delighters: Unexpected features that wow customers.
Why It Matters:
Helps prioritize based on customer experience impact.
Benefits of Using Prioritization Frameworks

Ensures resources focus on high-impact initiatives.
Improves stakeholder alignment by making prioritization transparent.
Reduces risk of wasted effort on low-value projects.
