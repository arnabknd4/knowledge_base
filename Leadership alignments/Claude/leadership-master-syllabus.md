## PART L1: HOW THIS DOCUMENT FITS THE MASTER SYLLABUS

Purpose: the master syllabus covers technology and D15 covers architecture practice at a basic level. This document is the full leadership track. It uses the same note template, the same tags, the same phases (P1 to P4) and the same batch loop. Nothing here replaces the master. It adds to it.

Rules:

- Domains continue the master numbering: D25 to D32. IDs run 25.01 to 32.04, so they never clash with master IDs 1.01 to 24.03.
- The master topics 15.01 to 15.08 and 17.01 to 17.05 stay as they are. Leadership topics add depth and never repeat them. Each line ends with "Links:" showing the master topics it builds on.
- Same loop as the master: take the next topic IDs from Part L5, send them to the Claude Project chat, skim, paste into the domain Word file, print, recall once on paper.
- Same gap-list rule: a concept not in the topic line goes on the domain gap list, never into extra browsing.
- Leadership is learned by doing. Every FULL note ends with a Practice exercise (in place of the mini lab). Do it on real work and record it in the practice log (Part L6). The log feeds the story bank (17.01).
- Pace: same as master, about 6 notes a month. Leadership notes are slotted into master batches (Part L5), about 3 per batch, so no separate schedule is needed.

Tags (same as master):

- P1, P2, P3, P4: phase in which to write the note.
- R = required for job readiness. O = optional.
- E = relevant to certification exams. L = used in real projects.
- K = you probably know most of this from 12 years of work. Generate, read once, fill gaps only.
- (verify) = laws, framework versions, exam names, salary ranges or tool names that change. Check the official source.

Note size: R topics FULL (about 700 words, one printed page). O topics SHORT (about 300 words, half a page).

## PART L2: NOTE TEMPLATE FOR LEADERSHIP TOPICS

Same 11 sections as master Part 2, with these changes:

- Section 4 Features and core concepts: for each concept give the plain meaning, why it matters, and one reusable tool (a template, agenda, phrase or checklist). No code.
- Section 6 Real-world use case: set in a .NET and Azure delivery situation with a client, an offshore team or a growing team. Include the trade-off and when NOT to apply it.
- Section 7 Interview lens: four questions with two-line answers. Mix behavioral ("tell me about a time") and managerial or architect-round scenario questions.
- Section 8 Common mistakes: how each shows up in a team, and the fix.
- Section 10 becomes Practice exercise (20 to 40 minutes, on real work): exact steps, the result you should have, what to record in the practice log. Skipped for K and O topics.
- Section 3 Before you read: skipped for K topics.
- SHORT note (O): sections 1, 4, 6 and 11 only.

Three passes per topic:

- Pass 1, paper, day 1: read the printed note, close it, handwrite the recall box ("In my own words: ... / I would use it when: ... / I would avoid it when: ...").
- Pass 2, within 3 days: do the Practice exercise once.
- Pass 3, within 2 weeks: use it in real work (a 1:1, a design review, a status report) and log the result in the practice log.
- Revise on day 3, day 10 and day 30 using only the revision summary.
