# Master Syllabus: Leadership for Technical Architect → Solution Architect → Enterprise Architect
Version: 1.0 | Prepared: 17 October 2026
Purpose: A practical, evidence-based leadership curriculum for a senior technologist who wants to operate effectively at technical, solution, and enterprise-architecture levels.

## How to use this syllabus

This is a companion to your Technical Architect syllabus, not a replacement for it. Your existing syllabus already includes technical leadership, communication and influence, delivery management, enterprise-architecture foundations, trade-off analysis, architecture governance, career stories, and architecture documentation. This curriculum expands those into a complete leadership operating system.

**Non-negotiable rule:** Every required topic must produce evidence of application. Reading and certificates alone do not count as competence.

### Tags
- **P1 — Lead yourself and delivery:** essential for senior engineer/technical lead.
- **P2 — Lead people and cross-functional outcomes:** essential for lead/principal/solution architect.
- **P3 — Lead strategy, portfolios, and change:** essential for enterprise architect and architecture leadership.
- **P4 — Executive/enterprise mastery:** advanced practice and sustained organizational influence.
- **R — Required:** complete the practice artifact and demonstrate application.
- **O — Optional/specialist:** do after required topics or when your target role requires it.
- **W — Workplace practice:** apply in a real project, with appropriate confidentiality.
- **V — Verify:** check current official framework or legal requirements before relying on version-specific details.

## The learning loop

For every R topic:
1. Write a one-page note in your own words: what it is, why it matters, when to use/avoid it, failure modes, and a real example.
2. Rehearse one conversation or decision aloud.
3. Create the named work product (memo, plan, agenda, scorecard, decision record, or retrospective).
4. Apply it at work or in a realistic case within two weeks.
5. Ask one trusted peer/manager/stakeholder for specific feedback.
6. Review after 3, 10, and 30 days. Keep evidence and record what changed.

Do not create confidential work artifacts in a public portfolio. Redact names, customer information, commercial terms, and security details.

## Competency standard

For each R topic, score yourself 0–4:
- 0: cannot explain it.
- 1: can explain terminology, but cannot apply it.
- 2: can apply with a template or coaching.
- 3: can apply independently and explain trade-offs.
- 4: can coach others and improve the organizational practice.

A topic is complete only at level 3 or above, supported by an artifact or observed behavior. Level 4 is a longer-term target.

# PART C — CAPSTONE: LEAD A REALISTIC ENTERPRISE CHANGE

Choose a real, suitably redacted initiative or a realistic case such as modernizing a legacy business application into a cloud-enabled platform. The goal is to demonstrate leadership, not just architecture knowledge.

Deliver these artifacts:
1. **Business problem brief:** customer/business problem, current baseline, desired outcomes, constraints, non-goals.
2. **Stakeholder and decision map:** affected groups, interests, decision owners, escalation paths.
3. **Options and investment memo:** at least three options including do-nothing; cost/risk/time/skills trade-offs; recommendation; assumptions.
4. **Architecture decision record:** context, options, decision, consequences, owner, revisit trigger.
5. **Roadmap and transition plan:** stages, dependencies, migration waves, rollback, adoption and decommissioning.
6. **Risk register:** technical, delivery, security, vendor, operational, people and adoption risks; owner and residual risk.
7. **Delivery operating model:** decision rights, governance cadence, escalation, quality gates, communications.
8. **People plan:** delegation, mentoring, capability gaps, onboarding, succession/key-person risk.
9. **Executive update:** one page, outcome status, forecast confidence, key risks, decision needed.
10. **Measurement plan:** baseline, target, data source, owner, review date, benefit realization.
11. **Incident/postmortem simulation:** lead the communication, timeline, learning, and corrective actions.
12. **Retrospective:** what changed, stakeholder feedback, what did not work, next iteration.

**Capstone acceptance criteria**
- The recommendation is tied to a measurable business outcome.
- Trade-offs and rejected alternatives are explicit.
- Every major risk has an owner and a response.
- Decision rights and escalation paths are clear.
- People/adoption work is included, not appended at the end.
- Metrics cannot be “green” merely because tasks were completed.
- The plan includes operational ownership, recovery, and decommissioning.
- You can present the recommendation in 5 minutes to a non-technical executive and defend it for 20 minutes with technical peers.

# PART D — WEEKLY OPERATING RHYTHM

A repeatable week:
- 45 minutes: study one or two topic notes.
- 45 minutes: practice a communication, negotiation, or decision scenario.
- 60–90 minutes: create or improve one artifact.
- 30 minutes: apply the skill at work or run a simulation.
- 15 minutes: capture evidence and feedback.

Monthly:
- Review competency scores and evidence.
- Ask one peer and one stakeholder for behavior-specific feedback.
- Select one improvement experiment.
- Review job descriptions for your intended next role; do not let job-market noise constantly change the syllabus.

Quarterly:
- Present a leadership case study to a peer/mentor.
- Review measurable outcomes and stakeholder feedback.
- Update your portfolio and career narrative.
- Choose specialist modules only if they close a demonstrated role gap.

# PART F — INDUSTRY FRAMEWORKS AND REFERENCE POINTS

Use frameworks as reference models, not as substitutes for judgement or practice.

- **TOGAF Standard, 10th Edition / The Open Group:** enterprise architecture method, domains, governance, capability thinking, roadmaps, and architecture practice. Official source: https://www.opengroup.org/togaf
- **ArchiMate:** a modeling language for relationships across business, application, and technology architecture. Official source: https://www.opengroup.org/archimate-forum/archimate-overview
- **PMI Talent Triangle:** ways of working, power skills, and business acumen; useful for balancing delivery, interpersonal, and commercial capabilities. Official source: https://www.pmi.org/certifications/certification-resources/maintain/talent-triangle
- **DORA research and software delivery metrics:** use delivery and reliability metrics as system-level learning signals, not individual performance rankings. Official source: https://dora.dev/
- **Google re:Work / manager research:** use manager behaviors and coaching resources as supplementary practice material. Official source: https://rework.withgoogle.com/
- **Center for Creative Leadership:** leadership development, feedback, coaching, and leading change. Official source: https://www.ccl.org/

Frameworks are not all formal standards, and no single framework covers all leadership competencies. Match the framework to the problem and your organization's context. Verify certification names, exam versions, and legal requirements from official sources before paying for training or relying on them.

# PART G — RULES THAT PROTECT THE PLAN

1. Do not treat confidence, charisma, seniority, or technical brilliance as substitutes for leadership evidence.
2. Do not pursue every certificate. First identify the role and the behavior gap it would address.
3. Do not use metrics to rank individuals without context or to create incentives for gaming.
4. Do not promise dates or budgets without assumptions, ranges, and confidence.
5. Do not call a meeting successful unless a decision, shared understanding, or useful next action resulted.
6. Do not label disagreement as resistance before understanding the concern.
7. Do not delegate responsibility without decision rights, support, and escalation clarity.
8. Do not use “blameless” to mean consequence-free; focus accountability on learning and system improvement.
9. Do not present AI-generated claims as verified facts; validate high-impact decisions.
10. Do not confuse an architecture board's approval with business ownership of the risk.
11. Do not publish employer or customer confidential material in a portfolio.
12. Do not skip practice. Leadership is demonstrated through repeated behavior in real situations.

## Completion definition

You are ready to credibly pursue a broader leadership role when you can show repeated evidence that you:
- make and communicate sound decisions under uncertainty;
- develop people and distribute ownership;
- align technical choices to business outcomes;
- negotiate scope, resources, and risk transparently;
- lead cross-functional delivery and change;
- establish proportionate governance and architecture guardrails;
- learn from failures without hiding them;
- influence senior stakeholders with concise evidence;
- measure outcomes rather than activity; and
- build leaders and systems that do not depend on you being the hero.
