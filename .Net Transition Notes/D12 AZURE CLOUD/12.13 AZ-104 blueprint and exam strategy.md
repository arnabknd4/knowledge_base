1 What is it

AZ-104 is the Microsoft Azure Administrator exam. It tests whether you can set up and run Azure resources day to day. Passing it earns the Azure Administrator Associate certification.

The blueprint is the official "skills measured" list. It names the domains and the percentage of questions each one gets.

Legacy equivalent: a Windows Server or MCSA administrator exam, but for cloud resources instead of servers on your own network.

2 Why needed

- It is the Phase 1 credential in your plan and the first proof of Azure skill for interviews.
- It forces you through identity, storage, compute, networking and monitoring once, end to end.
- Without a blueprint you study what you like, not what is scored.
- Without a strategy you can know the material and still fail on time or on question format.

4 Features and core concepts

- Skills outline domains (verify weights on Microsoft Learn): identity and governance, storage, compute, virtual networking, monitoring and backup. This is the scored list, so it is the study checklist. Compute and identity/governance usually weigh the most (verify).
- As currently published (verify): Microsoft updates the outline a few times a year. Always download the latest PDF before you plan. Study what is current, not what a video from two years ago says.
- Labs per domain: one small hands-on task per domain, done in a real subscription. This matters because the exam asks "which setting or command" questions that reading alone does not fix.
- Practice assessments: free official practice questions on Microsoft Learn. They show your weak domains and the exam question style.
- Time management: the exam has a fixed time limit and mixed question types, such as multiple choice, drag and drop, and case studies (verify count and duration). Some sections may not allow going back to earlier questions. Never spend more than about two minutes on one question on the first pass.
- Yearly renewal assessment: the certification is valid for one year. You renew by taking a free online assessment on Microsoft Learn before it expires (verify window). It is not a full exam.
- Memorize this: passing score is 700 on a scale of 1 to 1000 (verify).

5 Core focus (exam and interview)

- Most asked: RBAC scopes and role assignment, storage redundancy and SAS, VM and App Service basics, VNet peering, NSG rules, private endpoint, Azure Monitor alerts, Azure Backup.
- Expect "which tool or setting" and "what is the least effort or least privilege option" questions.
- Know both portal and CLI or PowerShell steps for common tasks.
- Interviewers use AZ-104 topics as a floor check: what a managed identity is, how NSGs differ from firewalls, LRS vs ZRS.

6 Real-world use case

Situation: your team must hand a production subscription to an operations group.

Choice: use the blueprint domains as a handover checklist. Check RBAC and locks, storage redundancy, VM backups, NSG rules and alerts.

Trade-off: the exam is broad and shallow. It proves admin coverage, not architecture depth.

When NOT to use it: if you will only design systems and never operate them, AZ-305 matters more. Even then, AZ-104 is still useful groundwork.

7 Interview lens

- Q: How did you prepare for AZ-104?
  A: I used the published skills outline as a checklist. I did one lab per domain and fixed weak domains using practice assessments.
- Q: Why does the exam need current outline checks?
  A: Azure services and weights change. Old guides can teach retired or renamed features.
- Q: How do you renew it?
  A: Take the free renewal assessment on Microsoft Learn before the one-year expiry.
- Q: How do you handle time in the exam?
  A: First pass fast, mark hard questions, return if allowed. Manage case studies separately.

8 Common mistakes

- Studying from an old video. Symptom: questions on features you never saw. Fix: download the current outline first.
- Reading only. Symptom: you cannot choose between similar settings. Fix: one lab per domain.
- Ignoring weak domains. Symptom: a low score in one section drags you under 700. Fix: use the practice assessment results.
- Spending too long on one question. Symptom: rushing the last ones. Fix: a time cap per question.
- Forgetting renewal. Symptom: expired certificate. Fix: calendar reminder at six months.

10 Mini lab

Time: 30 minutes. Needs an Azure free account and Azure CLI or Cloud Shell.

- Step 1: Open the AZ-104 study guide page on Microsoft Learn. Note the five domains, their weights and the last updated date.
- Step 2: Take the free practice assessment. Write your score per domain on paper.
- Step 3: Pick your weakest domain. Run these commands:
  az group create -n rg-lab -l centralindia
  az tag update --resource-id $(az group show -n rg-lab --query id -o tsv) --operations merge --tags env=lab
  az lock create -n nodelete --lock-type CanNotDelete -g rg-lab
- Expected result: the resource group exists with the env tag and a lock. Deleting it fails until the lock is removed.
- Clean-up: az lock delete -n nodelete -g rg-lab, then az group delete -n rg-lab --yes
