12.15 AZ-305 blueprint and design drills [P4 R E] — treat as new (no K tag), FULL note.

1 What is it

- AZ-305 is the Microsoft exam for Azure Solutions Architect Expert. It tests whether you can choose the right Azure services for a business need and justify the choice.
- It is a design exam, not a build exam. You read a scenario and pick the best option on cost, availability, security and effort.
- Design drills are timed practice sessions where you pick a service for a scenario, say why, and name what you rejected.
- Legacy equivalent: the architecture review you do before a project, where you choose IIS, SQL Server, MSMQ or WCF and defend the choice.

2 Why needed

- Passing proves you can reason about Azure trade-offs, which architect interviews test directly.
- Without drills you know the services but freeze when two options both seem right.
- This is the Phase 4 capstone for the cloud architect goal.

3 Before you read

- Azure basics from AZ-104: identity, networking, compute, storage.
- RPO (how much data loss is acceptable) and RTO (how long recovery may take).
- SLA: the uptime percentage Microsoft promises for a service.
- Well-Architected Framework: Microsoft's five quality pillars for any design.

4 Features and core concepts

Skills measured as of April 17, 2026 (verify on the official study guide). The weights are from the Microsoft Learn page.

- Design identity, governance and monitoring (25 to 30 percent): logging and routing logs, authentication, authorization to Azure and on-premises resources, secrets and keys, management groups, subscriptions, tagging, compliance, identity governance. Why it matters: this is the second biggest block and every design depends on it.
- Design data storage (20 to 25 percent): relational data, tier and compute choice, scalability, protection; semi-structured and unstructured data; data integration and analysis. Why it matters: the wrong database or tier is the most expensive mistake.
- Design business continuity (15 to 20 percent): backup and recovery for compute, databases and unstructured data; high availability for compute, relational and unstructured data. Why it matters: questions always give an RPO or RTO to meet.
- Design infrastructure (30 to 35 percent): compute (VMs, containers, serverless, batch), application architecture (messaging, events, API integration, caching, configuration, automated deployment), migrations, and network design. Why it matters: this is the largest block.
- Service-choice comparison sheets: one page each for storage, databases, compute, messaging, networking and identity. Each lists the options, when to pick each, and the deciding factor. Why it matters: this turns memorized services into quick decisions.
- Case-study reasoning: read requirements, mark the constraint words (cheapest, lowest effort, no code change, must survive a region failure), then pick.
- Cost and SLA trade-offs: more nines and more regions cost more. Always check whether the requirement justifies the price.

5 Core focus (exam and interview)

- Questions ask for the best option, not a working one. The qualifier decides the answer.
- A score of 700 or more passes.
- The certification must be renewed every year with a free online assessment on Microsoft Learn.
- Preview features may appear if commonly used (verify).
- Prefer managed (PaaS) services unless the scenario demands control.

6 Real-world use case

- Situation: an IIS-hosted .NET Framework order system, SQL Server, MSMQ, must survive a zone failure.
- Choice: App Service with zone redundancy, Azure SQL with zone-redundant tier, Service Bus Premium for messaging.
- Trade-off: higher monthly cost and some code change for MSMQ to Service Bus.
- When not to use: for a small internal tool with a long outage allowed, single-zone Standard tiers are cheaper and enough.

7 Interview lens

- How do you pick between Service Bus, Event Grid and Event Hubs? Commands and ordered work go to Service Bus, reactions to state changes go to Event Grid, high-volume telemetry streams go to Event Hubs.
- How do you design for RPO of zero? Use synchronous replication such as zone-redundant storage or databases. Cross-region synchronous copies cost latency, so say that.
- Why choose App Service over AKS? Less operations work. Pick AKS only when you need Kubernetes control or portability.
- How do you handle a migration with no code change allowed? Rehost to VMs or use Managed Instance for SQL Server compatibility.

8 Common mistakes

- Choosing the most powerful service. The exam rewards the simplest that meets the need.
- Missing a constraint word. Fix: underline cost, effort and availability words first.
- Confusing backup with high availability. Backup restores data; HA keeps service running.
- Studying services one by one instead of comparing them. Fix: use the comparison sheets.

9 Mental model

- Scenario in, then constraints out, then shortlist of two, then the constraint picks the winner.
- Requirement words map to levers: availability to zones and regions, cost to tiers and PaaS, effort to managed services, security to private access and identity.

10 Mini lab (about 40 minutes)

- Step 1: open the free AZ-305 practice assessment on Microsoft Learn and take 15 questions.
- Step 2: for each wrong answer, write the constraint word you missed.
- Step 3: open the Azure pricing calculator and price the use case in section 6 two ways: single zone and zone redundant.
- Step 4: write one comparison sheet line for each service you used.
- Expected result: a list of missed constraint words and a cost gap between the two designs.
- Clean-up: delete the calculator estimate. No Azure resources are created.

11 Revision summary

- Four domains: identity and governance 25 to 30, data 20 to 25, continuity 15 to 20, infrastructure 30 to 35 percent.
