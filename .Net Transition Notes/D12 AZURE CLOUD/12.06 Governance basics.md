1 What is it
- Azure governance is the set of controls that keep an Azure estate organized, compliant and affordable as more teams and subscriptions are added.
- Five parts: Azure Policy (rules on resources), management groups (a hierarchy above subscriptions), tags (labels for cost and ownership), locks (protection from delete or change), and Cost Management with Advisor (budgets, alerts, recommendations). Defender for Cloud overview adds a security view.
- The Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF) is Microsoft's staged guide for adopting Azure. Its stages are Strategy, Plan, Ready, Migrate, Innovate, Govern, Manage (verify).
- Legacy equivalent: Active Directory Group Policy plus IT change control, naming standards and the server inventory spreadsheet, applied to cloud resources.

2 Why needed
- Without governance, any developer can create any resource in any region at any size.
- Production breaks when someone deletes a resource group by mistake.
- Bills grow with no owner to ask, because nothing is tagged.
- Audits fail because there is no proof that encryption or region rules were followed.
- Rules done by email and review do not scale. Governance enforces them automatically.

3 Before you read
- Subscription and resource group: a subscription is a billing and access boundary, a resource group is a container for related resources.
- Azure RBAC: controls who can do what. Policy controls what can exist.
- ARM (Azure Resource Manager): the deployment layer through which every create or update request passes.
- JSON basics: policy definitions are written in JSON.

4 Features and core concepts
Azure Policy
- Policy definition: a rule saying what is allowed or not, for example "only allow regions Central India and South India". It matters because the rule is checked on every create and update.
- Initiative: a group of policy definitions assigned together, for example a compliance baseline. It matters because you manage one assignment, not fifty.
- Assignment: applies a definition or initiative at a scope (management group, subscription, resource group).
- Effects: what happens on a match. Common ones: Deny (block), Audit (flag only), Modify (change properties, such as add tags), Append, DeployIfNotExists (create a missing related resource, such as a diagnostic setting), Disabled. Pick Audit first to see impact, then Deny.
- Remediation: a task that fixes existing resources that were created before the policy. Modify and DeployIfNotExists apply to new resources automatically, existing ones need a remediation task. It matters because policy is not retroactive for changes by default.
- Compliance view: shows which resources pass or fail.

Management groups
- A tree above subscriptions that lets you apply policy and RBAC once and inherit downward. It matters because dozens of subscriptions cannot be managed one by one.
- Limits to remember: up to 6 levels of depth excluding the root and the subscription level (verify). One root management group per tenant.

Tagging strategy
- Tags are name-value pairs on resources, for example CostCenter, Owner, Environment, Application. They matter for cost allocation, automation and ownership.
- Tags are not inherited from resource group to resource by default. Use a Modify policy (or the built-in "inherit tag from resource group" policies) to copy them.
- Plan a small mandatory set and enforce it with policy.

Locks
- CanNotDelete: can read and modify, cannot delete. ReadOnly: can read only.
- Locks apply to everyone, including Owners, until removed. Locks override RBAC.
- ReadOnly can break things unexpectedly, for example a storage account lock can block listing keys. Prefer CanNotDelete for production.

Defender for Cloud overview
- Gives a secure score, recommendations and threat protection across subscriptions. The free tier gives posture recommendations. Paid Defender plans add workload protection (verify).

Cost Management, budgets and alerts
- Cost analysis shows spend by resource, tag, resource group or service.
- Budgets set a spend limit and trigger alerts at percentage thresholds. A budget alerts, it does not stop resources by itself.
- Action groups can call automation when a budget alert fires.

Azure Advisor
- Free recommendations in five categories: Reliability, Security, Performance, Cost, Operational Excellence. It matters for right-sizing and finding idle resources.

Cloud adoption framework stages
- Strategy (why), Plan (what), Ready (landing zone), Adopt (migrate and innovate), Govern, Manage (verify the current stage names).
- Governance is a stage of its own and also runs through all of them.

Worth memorizing
- az policy assignment create --name allowed-regions --policy <definition-id> --scope <scope> --params '{...}'
- az lock create --name protect --lock-type CanNotDelete --resource-group rg-prod

5 Core focus (exam and interview)
- RBAC vs Policy: RBAC answers who can act, Policy answers what is allowed. A user with Owner can still be blocked by Deny policy.
- Deny vs Audit: Deny blocks creation, Audit only records non-compliance.
- Policy inheritance flows down from management group to subscription to resource group to resource. Exclusions can be set at assignment.
- Tags do not inherit by default.
- Locks apply regardless of role, and a lock on a resource group is inherited by its resources.
- Moving a resource between subscriptions or groups can be blocked by locks.
- Budgets notify only. Cost alerts can be delayed, because billing data is not real-time (verify the delay).
- Policy evaluation: on create or update, and a full compliance scan about every 24 hours (verify).

6 Real-world use case
Situation: a Kolkata team of 40 developers on an Infosys project gets 6 subscriptions (dev, test, prod across two applications). Costs are growing and nobody knows which team owns what.
Choice made:
- Management groups: Platform, NonProd, Prod.
- Policy at management group level: allowed regions (India only), mandatory tags Owner and CostCenter, deny public IP on non-gateway resources, audit unencrypted storage.
- Modify policy copies the CostCenter tag from the resource group.
- CanNotDelete lock on production resource groups.
- Budgets per subscription with 80 percent and 100 percent alerts to the team mailbox.
Trade-off: strict Deny policies slow developers when they hit a block in dev. Policies also add maintenance effort.
When not to use it: in a single small subscription with two people, heavy initiatives and a management group tree are overhead. Use a few tags, one budget and basic locks. Do not use ReadOnly locks on resources that need to write to themselves, such as App Service plans with autoscale.

7 Interview lens
Q1: What is the difference between Azure Policy and RBAC?
- RBAC controls what a user may do on a resource. Policy controls what properties a resource may have, no matter who creates it.
Q2: How do you make sure every resource has a CostCenter tag?
- Assign a Modify or Deny policy at a management group. Use Modify to inherit from the resource group and Deny to block untagged creation.
Q3: A policy was assigned but old resources show non-compliant. What now?
- Policy shows them but does not fix them. Run a remediation task for Modify or DeployIfNotExists policies, or fix them manually.
Q4: Does a budget stop spending when the limit is hit?
- No. A budget sends alerts. To act on it, connect an action group to automation that stops or scales down resources.

8 Common mistakes
- Assigning Deny policy straight to production. Deployments fail at release time. Fix: use Audit first, review compliance, then switch to Deny.
- Expecting tags to inherit. Cost reports show untagged spend. Fix: use inherit-tag policies.
- ReadOnly lock on a resource group. Pipelines fail with confusing errors. Fix: use CanNotDelete unless you really need read-only.
- Assigning policy at subscription level everywhere. Rules drift between subscriptions. Fix: assign at management group and use initiatives.
- Budgets with no alert recipients. Overspend goes unnoticed. Fix: always attach an action group or email contacts.
- Ignoring Advisor. Idle VMs and oversize SKUs run for months. Fix: review Advisor monthly.

Policy is the building code, RBAC is the key card, locks are the padlock, tags are the labels, budgets are the electricity meter alerts.

10 Mini lab (about 30 minutes, Azure free tier, Cloud Shell)
Steps:
- Create a resource group: az group create -n rg-gov-lab -l centralindia
- In the portal open Policy, Definitions, search "Require a tag and its value on resource groups" (or "Allowed locations"). Assign "Allowed locations" to rg-gov-lab with only Central India selected.
- Try: az storage account create -n govlab<random> -g rg-gov-lab -l eastus --sku Standard_LRS
- Expected result: request denied with a RequestDisallowedByPolicy error.
- Change the assignment effect to Audit (use the "Audit" variant of the built-in policy if offered) and create the same storage account in eastus. Wait about 15 to 30 minutes, then check Policy, Compliance. Expected result: the account appears as non-compliant (verify timing).
- Add a lock: az lock create -n nodelete --lock-type CanNotDelete -g rg-gov-lab
- Try: az group delete -n rg-gov-lab. Expected result: delete is blocked by the lock.
- In Cost Management create a budget of a small amount on the resource group with an 80 percent email alert.
Clean-up:
- az lock delete --name nodelete --resource-group rg-gov-lab
- Delete the policy assignment in the portal.
- az group delete -n rg-gov-lab --yes --no-wait
