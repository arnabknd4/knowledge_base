1 What is it
- FinOps is the practice of managing cloud spend so that engineering, finance and business teams share responsibility for cost.
- Cost management is the set of Azure tools and habits that show, limit and reduce what you pay.
- Pricing calculator: a web tool that estimates the monthly cost of a design before you build it.
- TCO (total cost of ownership): the full cost of running something over years, including hardware, licences, power, people and migration, not just the monthly bill.
- Tags: name-value labels on resources, for example CostCenter=Retail. Cost allocation means splitting the bill by those labels.
- Budgets and anomaly alerts: a budget is a spending limit with notifications. An anomaly alert fires when spend departs from its normal pattern.
- Showback: show each team what it spent, without charging. Chargeback: actually bill the team internally.
- Legacy equivalent: the yearly on-premises hardware budget and server capacity planning, where you bought for peak load and paid whether used or not.

2 Why needed
- Cloud is pay as you go. Anyone with access can create cost in minutes.
- Without tags, budgets and review, the bill arrives as one number nobody owns.
- Typical failures: forgotten dev VMs, oversized databases, data leaving Azure at egress rates, unused reservations.
- Architects are expected to justify a design in money as well as in performance.

4 Features and core concepts
- Pricing calculator: add services, pick region, size and hours, get a monthly estimate. Why: gives a number for proposals and design reviews. Prices change, so always re-check (verify).
- TCO: compare current on-premises cost with Azure cost over three to five years. Include people, data centre, licence and migration cost. Why: this is the business case for a migration (12.09). Azure has a TCO or migration business-case tool, check the current name (verify).
- Tags and cost allocation: apply tags like CostCenter, Owner, Environment, App. Why: Cost Management can group and filter spend by tag, so each team sees its share. Policy can enforce required tags (12.06). Tags are not inherited by default (verify).
- Budgets: a monthly or quarterly amount at subscription or resource group scope, with alerts at percentages such as 50, 80 and 100. Why: early warning. A budget alerts but does not stop spend by itself, unless you attach an action group that runs automation.
- Reservations and savings plans: commit to one or three years of use for a discount. Reservation: fixed service and size, biggest discount. Savings plan: commit to an hourly spend, more flexible across services. Why: steady workloads cost much less. Discount figures vary, so do not memorize numbers (verify).
- Right-sizing: pick the smallest size that meets measured need. Why: most resources are oversized. Azure Advisor gives cost recommendations from usage data.
- Autoscale: add and remove instances by load. Why: pay for peak only when peak happens.
- Dev-test pricing: lower rates for dev and test subscriptions. Why: non-production does not need production prices. Eligibility rules apply (verify).
- Auto-shutdown: schedule VMs to stop at night. Why: stopped (deallocated) VMs stop compute charges. Disks still cost.
- Storage tiering: hot, cool, cold, archive blob tiers and lifecycle rules. Why: old data moves to cheaper tiers automatically.
- Egress costs: data leaving an Azure region or going to the internet is charged. Why: chatty cross-region designs and large downloads surprise teams.
- FinOps framework: Inform (visibility, allocation, reporting), Optimize (rates and usage), Operate (governance, continuous process).
- Anomaly alerts: notify on unusual spend jumps. Why: catches runaway loops, leaked keys and mistakes within a day.
- Showback and chargeback: showback reports cost per team. Chargeback bills it. Why: teams that see or pay their cost change behaviour.
- Worth memorizing: az consumption budget create (verify exact syntax) and Cost Management scopes: management group, subscription, resource group.

5 Core focus (exam and interview)
- Most asked: how to cut cost without hurting reliability. Answer in order: remove waste, right-size, autoscale, then commit with reservations or savings plans.
- Reservation versus savings plan: reservation is less flexible and cheaper, savings plan is flexible.
- Budgets alert, they do not block.
- Tags enable allocation but need enforcement through Azure Policy.
- Deallocated VMs do not bill for compute, but disks and public IPs may.
- Hybrid benefit lets you reuse existing Windows Server and SQL Server licences. This matters for your legacy estate (verify terms).
- Spot VMs are cheap but can be evicted, so only for interruptible work.
- Cost Management scopes and the Cost Management Reader role (verify) exist for read-only access.

6 Real-world use case
- Situation: a migrated .NET Framework app on VMs plus Azure SQL costs more than expected. Finance sees one shared subscription bill.
- Choice: enforce CostCenter and Environment tags by Policy, split dev and prod into separate subscriptions with budgets, auto-shutdown dev VMs, right-size using Advisor, and buy a one-year reservation only for the stable production database and VMs after three months of data.
- Trade-off: commitments save money but lock you in. If the app is retired or redesigned, you still pay.
- When NOT to use: do not buy reservations for unproven or short-lived workloads. Do not chargeback before tagging is reliable, because wrong numbers destroy trust.

7 Interview lens
- Q: Your Azure bill grew 40 percent this month. What do you do?
- A: Open Cost Management, group by service, resource group and tag to find the change. Check anomaly alerts and recent deployments, then fix the cause and add a budget.
- Q: Reservation or savings plan?
- A: Reservation for fixed, stable resources at the highest discount. Savings plan when services or sizes may change.
- Q: How do you make teams own cost?
- A: Enforce tags, give each team a dashboard (showback), set budgets with owners, review monthly. Move to chargeback when data is trusted.
- Q: How do you estimate cost of a new design?
- A: Pricing calculator with assumptions on size, hours, data volume and egress. Document the assumptions in an ADR and recheck after launch.

8 Common mistakes
- Untagged resources: report shows a large "unallocated" slice. Fix: Policy to require tags, and a backfill.
- Budgets with no owner or email: alerts go nowhere. Fix: action groups to the team channel.
- Reserving too early: unused commitment. Fix: wait for stable usage data.
- Forgetting non-VM costs: disks, snapshots, public IPs, egress, log ingestion. Fix: review the full resource list.
- Keeping dev and test at production size, running 24 hours. Fix: smaller SKUs and auto-shutdown.
- Treating cost as a one-time exercise. Fix: monthly review cycle.

9 Mental model
Inform -> Optimize -> Operate, repeated.
See it (tags, reports) -> trim it (waste, size, scale, commit) -> keep it (budgets, alerts, reviews).
Rule of thumb: remove, shrink, scale, then commit.

10 Mini lab
- Time: about 30 minutes. Azure free tier, no paid resources.
- Step 1: open the Azure pricing calculator and estimate one App Service plan plus one Azure SQL database for a month. Note the total.
- Step 2: in the portal, create a resource group named rg-finops-lab and tag it CostCenter=Lab, Environment=Dev.
- Step 3: open Cost Management, Budgets, create a budget of a small amount (for example 5 units of your currency) on that resource group, with alerts at 50 and 100 percent to your email.
- Step 4: open Cost analysis, scope it to your subscription, group by Tag, then by Resource group. Note that new data can take hours to appear.
- Step 5: open Advisor and read the Cost tab, if any recommendations exist.
- Expected result: a budget exists with alerts, the resource group shows its tags, and you have a written estimate to compare with real cost later.
- Clean-up: delete the resource group rg-finops-lab, which removes the budget scope resources, then delete the budget if it remains.

11 Revision summary
- FinOps phases: Inform, Optimize, Operate.
- Order of savings: remove waste, right-size, autoscale, then commit.
- Reservation is cheaper and rigid. Savings plan is flexible.
- Budgets alert only. They do not stop spend.
- Tags drive allocation. Enforce them with Azure Policy.
- Deallocated VM stops compute billing. Disks still bill.
- Egress and log ingestion are common hidden costs.
- Showback shows cost. Chargeback bills it.
