1 What is it
- Offer and negotiation is the skill of turning a job offer into the best total package, and comparing offers fairly. In India this means understanding CTC (Cost to Company), what actually reaches your bank account, notice period rules, and how to ask for more without losing the offer.
- Plain terms: CTC is the total yearly cost the employer books for you. In-hand is what you actually receive each month. They are not the same number.
- Legacy equivalent: In your current job, your salary slip and appraisal letter. This topic is the same slip, read critically before you sign.

2 Why needed
- Two offers with the same headline CTC can differ by lakhs in monthly in-hand pay.
- Without it, you may accept a number that includes retention bonuses, insurance premiums and employer PF you never see as cash.
- A lead moving to architect is judged partly on how they negotiate scope, level and title, not only pay.
- Without a plan, a counter-offer from your current employer or a long notice period can derail the move.

3 Before you read
- Your current salary slip with all components listed.
- Your current notice period clause in the appointment letter.
- A rough target role level (Lead, Senior Lead, Architect).
- Basic idea of income tax slabs under the new and old regime (verify current slabs).

4 Features and core concepts
- CTC structure: the total annual cost, split into fixed and variable parts. Why it matters: only the fixed part is guaranteed.
- Fixed pay: basic, HRA, special allowance and similar. Paid monthly regardless of performance. Why it matters: this drives your in-hand and loan eligibility.
- Variable pay: a bonus tied to company or individual performance. Often paid at less than 100 percent. Why it matters: ask for the actual payout history of the last two years, not the target.
- PF (Provident Fund): a retirement saving deducted from your pay, with a matching employer share. Why it matters: employer PF is inside CTC but you get it only later. Check whether PF is calculated on the full basic or on a capped amount (verify).
- Gratuity: a lump sum paid after five years of service, based on last basic and years worked (verify current rules). Why it matters: some companies count gratuity inside CTC, which inflates the headline number.
- Stock (ESOP or RSU): company shares granted over a vesting schedule. Why it matters: value depends on vesting, strike price, liquidity and tax. Unlisted company stock may be worth nothing in cash for years.
- In-hand vs CTC calculation: in-hand is roughly CTC minus employer PF, gratuity, insurance, variable not yet paid, employee PF and income tax, then divided by 12. Why it matters: this is the number your household budget and EMI depend on. Ask HR for a full monthly salary breakup and calculate it yourself.
- Notice period and buyout: the time you must serve before leaving, or a payment to leave early. Why it matters: 60 to 90 days is common for leads. A new employer may negotiate the buyout or fix a later joining date. Check whether buyout is allowed in your contract.
- Counter-offers: your current employer offers more money or a promotion to keep you. Why it matters: it can feel good but the reasons you wanted to leave usually remain. Decide your answer before you resign, not after.
- Comparing offers: judge total pay, growth, role content and stability together. Why it matters: a higher number for a maintenance role can hurt your architect path. Compare on fixed in-hand, learning, scope and team.
- Background verification (BGV): the employer checks your past employment, education and sometimes salary records. Why it matters: wrong dates or inflated pay can lead to offer withdrawal. Keep payslips, relieving letters and Form 16 ready.
- Negotiation scripts: short, polite wording to ask for more. Why it matters: it keeps the tone positive. Example: "I am excited about the role. Based on my 12 years and the scope you described, I was expecting a fixed pay closer to X. Can we look at that?"

5 Core focus (exam and interview)
- Asked most: how you handle a lower offer than expected, how you compare two offers, and what your expectation is.
- Never give your number first if you can avoid it. Ask for the range of the role, then respond.
- Always negotiate on fixed pay and level first, then joining bonus, variable guarantee, notice buyout and stock.
- Get everything in writing in the offer letter before you resign.
- Remember: PF, gratuity and insurance have rules and caps that change (verify).

6 Real-world use case
- Situation: You are a Lead at a service company with a 90-day notice. A product company in Bengaluru and a Kolkata firm both offer you Architect-track roles.
- Choice made: You build a one-page sheet with monthly in-hand, variable history, stock terms, notice buyout, role scope and growth path for each offer. You ask the better-fit company to cover buyout and move the joining date.
- Trade-off: The higher CTC offer has 30 percent variable and illiquid stock. The lower one has higher fixed pay and a clear architect title. You pick by in-hand stability and role content.
- When NOT to use hard negotiation: when the offer is already at or above your target and the company is small or has a firm band. Pushing too far can cost goodwill. Also avoid bluffing a fake competing offer.

7 Interview lens
- Q: What are your salary expectations?
  A: I would like to understand the role scope and band first. Based on my 12 years and the architect responsibilities, I am looking for a fixed pay in the range of X to Y.
- Q: How do you compare two offers?
  A: I compare fixed in-hand pay, variable payout history, stock liquidity, role scope and growth. Headline CTC alone is misleading.
- Q: What do you do if your employer gives a counter-offer?
  A: I decide before resigning. A counter-offer rarely fixes the reasons I wanted to leave, so I usually decline unless scope and level truly change.
- Q: How do you handle a long notice period?
  A: I check buyout options, ask the new employer to share or cover the cost, and negotiate a later joining date. I also plan a clean handover.

8 Common mistakes
- Accepting a headline CTC without a monthly breakup. Shows up as a lower in-hand than expected in month one. Fix: ask for the full salary structure before accepting.
- Counting target variable as guaranteed pay. Shows up as a shortfall when the bonus pays out at 60 percent. Fix: ask for payout history.
- Resigning before a signed offer letter. Shows up as a withdrawn offer and no job. Fix: resign only after the letter and BGV clearance.
- Hiding or inflating past salary. Shows up as BGV failure. Fix: share accurate numbers and documents.
- Naming your number too early. Shows up as a lower final offer. Fix: ask for the band first.
- Accepting a counter-offer on money alone. Shows up as the same problems six months later. Fix: write down your reasons for leaving before you decide.

9 Mental model
- Think of an offer as a table with three layers:

  Layer 1: Cash you get monthly (fixed in-hand)
  Layer 2: Cash that may come later (variable, bonus, PF, gratuity)
  Layer 3: Non-cash value (stock, title, learning, growth)

- Negotiate Layer 1 and the role level first. Treat Layers 2 and 3 as bonus, not as guaranteed pay.

10 Mini lab
- Time: about 30 minutes. Tools: a spreadsheet or paper.
- Steps:
  - List your current CTC components from your salary slip, one per row.
  - Mark each as fixed, variable or deferred (PF, gratuity, insurance).
  - Calculate your real monthly in-hand from the slip and compare it with CTC divided by 12.
  - Invent a second offer with a 20 percent higher CTC and 30 percent of it variable. Recalculate its expected in-hand.
  - Write your three negotiation asks in order of priority, plus a two-sentence script for each.
- Expected result: a one-page comparison showing that the higher CTC offer can have lower monthly in-hand.
- Clean-up: delete or shred any sheet containing real salary numbers if you shared the file anywhere.

11 Revision summary
- CTC is not in-hand; in-hand is what reaches your bank.
- Only fixed pay is guaranteed; ask for variable payout history.
- Employer PF and gratuity sit inside CTC but are paid later.
- Stock is valuable only if it vests and can be sold.
- Resign only after a signed offer letter and BGV clearance.
- Ask for the band first; do not name your number early.
- Negotiate fixed pay, level and notice buyout before smaller perks.
- Compare offers on in-hand, growth, role content and stability.
