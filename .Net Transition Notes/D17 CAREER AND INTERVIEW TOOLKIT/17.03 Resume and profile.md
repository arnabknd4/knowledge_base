1 What is it
- A lead-level resume and online profile set: one resume, one LinkedIn page, one GitHub profile and a few recruiter messages. All of them carry the same story.
- Resume = a 2-page document that shows scope, ownership and results. Profile = the public pages recruiters search (LinkedIn, GitHub). Keyword mapping = matching your wording to the words used in job postings, so search tools and recruiters find you.
- ATS (applicant tracking system) = software that stores resumes and filters them by keywords before a human reads them.
- Legacy equivalent: the Naukri profile and the "Responsibilities" list in your old resume, rewritten so each line shows impact instead of duties.

2 Why needed
- Recruiters spend under a minute on a first read. Lead-level roles are screened on scope, numbers and keywords.
- Without it, 12 years of work reads as "MVC, EF, SQL Server" and the Azure, microservices and architect direction stays invisible.
- Without keywords, the ATS or recruiter search never shows your profile for Azure DevOps, AKS or Terraform roles.
- Without referrals and outreach, you wait in a queue with hundreds of other applicants.

3 Before you read
- STAR stories from 17.01 (you need facts and numbers to put in bullets).
- Capstone project and its README from 17.02 (this is your proof for new skills).
- Two or three real job postings you want (Kolkata or remote India). Save them as text.

4 Features and core concepts
- Lead-level resume structure
  - Plain meaning: a fixed order that a recruiter can scan fast.
  - Why it matters: leads are hired for scope and judgment, so scope comes first.
  - Order: header, 3-line summary, skills grid, experience (newest first), capstone and certifications, education.
  - Length: two pages. Plain single-column layout, no tables, no icons, no photo (ATS can misread them).
  - Header: name, city, phone, email, LinkedIn URL, GitHub URL. Nothing else.
- Keyword mapping to postings (Azure DevOps, microservices, Terraform, AKS)
  - Plain meaning: copy the repeated nouns from 5 to 10 postings into a list, then use the true ones in your resume.
  - Why it matters: ATS and recruiter searches match exact terms.
  - Method: paste the postings into a file, count repeated terms, keep the top 15 to 20.
  - Use the exact spelling: "Azure DevOps", "Kubernetes (AKS)", "Terraform", "microservices", "CI/CD", "ASP.NET Core".
  - Rule: list only what you can explain in an interview. Put capstone-only skills under "Hands-on (project)" instead of hiding them as years of experience.
- Quantified achievements
  - Plain meaning: each bullet states action, scope and a number.
  - Why it matters: numbers separate a lead from a developer.
  - Formula: Did X by doing Y, resulting in Z.
  - Example shape: "Cut release time from 4 hours to 35 minutes by building a GitLab CI pipeline for 12 services."
  - Where numbers come from: team size, services, users, release frequency, defect rate, cost, time saved. Use your STAR stories. Estimate honestly and say "approx." if exact data is gone.
  - Keep 3 to 5 bullets per role. Older roles get fewer.
- LinkedIn headline and summary
  - Plain meaning: the headline is the 220-character line under your name. The About section is your short pitch.
  - Why it matters: recruiter search ranks on the headline and skills.
  - Headline shape: "Tech Lead | .NET, Azure, Microservices | Modernizing legacy .NET to cloud | Open to Architect roles".
  - About shape: who you are (1 line), what you have done with numbers (2 lines), what you are building now (1 line), what you want (1 line).
  - Also fill: Skills (top 3 pinned = .NET, Azure, Microservices), Featured (capstone link), Open to Work for recruiters only.
- GitHub profile
  - Plain meaning: your public code shelf.
  - Why it matters: it is the proof for skills not yet used in production.
  - Pin 3 to 4 repos. Capstone first.
  - Each pinned repo needs a README with an architecture diagram, how to run, and decisions (ADRs from 15.01).
  - Profile README: 5 lines only (role, stack, current focus, links).
  - Never push secrets or client code. Check this before making anything public.
- Recruiter outreach messages
  - Plain meaning: a short cold message to a recruiter or hiring manager.
  - Why it matters: many roles are filled before they are advertised widely.
  - Shape (under 80 words): who you are, one line of proof, the role you target, a clear ask, a link.
  - Send to 3 to 5 people per week. Follow up once after 5 to 7 days.
- Referrals
  - Plain meaning: an employee submits your resume internally.
  - Why it matters: referred candidates usually get a first screen much more often than cold applicants.
  - Method: list 20 ex-colleagues and connections at target firms. Send a short message with the job link, your resume and 2 lines on why you fit. Make it easy to say yes.
  - Thank them whatever the outcome.

5 Core focus (exam and interview)
- Most asked in screening: "Tell me about yourself", your biggest project, your team size and your role.
- Your resume is the interview script. Every bullet can be asked about. Do not list what you cannot explain.
- Recruiters check: current title, years of experience, notice period, current and expected CTC, location, skill match.
- Keep notice period and location visible on the profile or in the message.
- Limits to remember: resume 2 pages; LinkedIn headline 220 characters; outreach message under 80 words; 3 to 5 bullets per role.
- Save and send the resume as PDF unless the portal asks for Word. Name it ArnabSurname_TechLead_DotNet_Azure.pdf.

6 Real-world use case
- Situation: you apply to a .NET Architect opening. Your old resume opens with "Responsibilities: development, bug fixing, client calls".
- Choice: rewrite as an achievement-led resume. Summary line: "Tech lead, 12 years, legacy .NET modernization to Azure and microservices". Add Skills grid with exact posting terms. Add capstone and AZ-104.
- Trade-off: a tailored resume takes 20 to 30 minutes per application. A generic one takes zero but gets fewer replies.
- Approach: keep one master resume with everything, then make 2 or 3 variants (Azure/.NET Architect, DevOps-heavy lead, Microservices lead). Tweak the summary and top skills only.
- When NOT to do this: do not add Terraform, AKS or Kubernetes as "5 years" experience when it is lab or capstone work. Interviewers test the first follow-up question and the claim collapses. Label it honestly.

7 Interview lens
- Q: Walk me through your resume in two minutes.
  - A: Start with the present role and scope. Then two biggest wins with numbers, then your move toward Azure and architecture.
  - Close with what you want next. Do not read it line by line.
- Q: What was your biggest impact as a lead?
  - A: Pick one STAR story. Give the problem, your decision, the number and what changed after.
- Q: You list Terraform. Where did you use it?
  - A: State plainly: capstone project, with the repo link. Then describe state, modules and one mistake you fixed.
- Q: Why are you moving from lead to architect?
  - A: Say you already make design decisions and want it as the main job. Give one decision you owned and its trade-off.

8 Common mistakes
- Duty lists with no numbers: reader cannot see impact. Fix: rewrite with action, scope, result.
- Keyword stuffing of 60 tools: ATS passes it, the recruiter rejects it. Fix: 15 to 20 real terms.
- Fancy two-column templates with graphics: ATS drops text. Fix: plain single column.
- LinkedIn headline just "Tech Lead at Company": invisible in search. Fix: add the stack and target role.
- Empty GitHub or repos without README: looks unfinished. Fix: pin only the capstone and 2 good repos.
- Cold messages that say "any openings?": no reply. Fix: name the role and give one line of proof.
- Different dates or titles on resume and LinkedIn: background verification flags it. Fix: keep both identical.
- Client names or confidential data in bullets: breach of contract. Fix: use "a banking client" and no internal figures.

9 Mental model
- One story, four surfaces.

  Story bank (17.01) + Capstone (17.02)
          |
          v
  Master resume --> variants per role
          |
          +--> LinkedIn (search) --> recruiter finds you
          +--> GitHub (proof)    --> hiring manager checks you
          +--> Messages/referral --> a human opens the door

- Facts come from the story bank. Surfaces only change the format.

10 Mini lab
- Time: about 40 minutes. Tools: a text editor and Word or Google Docs. Free.
- Steps:
  - 1. Save 5 target job postings as text in one file.
  - 2. Highlight repeated nouns. Write the top 15 in a list. Mark each as "used at work", "used in capstone" or "not yet".
  - 3. Take your latest role. Write 4 bullets using the formula (action, scope, number). Take numbers from your story bank.
  - 4. Write the 3-line summary and the LinkedIn headline (under 220 characters).
  - 5. Write one outreach message under 80 words for one real role.
  - 6. Read all of it aloud. Mark every claim you could not defend in an interview.
- Expected result: a keyword list, 4 numeric bullets, a summary, a headline and one ready message.
- Clean-up: delete the saved posting file if it holds personal contact data. Keep the master resume file with a date in its name.

11 Revision summary
- Resume: two pages, single column, scope first, results with numbers.
- Bullet formula: action, scope, number.
- Keywords come from real postings; use only terms you can defend.
- LinkedIn headline: role, stack, target (max 220 characters).
- GitHub: pin 3 or 4 repos, capstone first, each with a README and diagram.
- Outreach: under 80 words, name the role, one proof line, one clear ask.
- Referral: send the job link, resume and 2 lines of fit; thank them.
- Label lab and capstone skills honestly; keep resume and LinkedIn identical.
